## Claims

### Q1 — Amendments, the January 2026 proposal, commencement dates

1. **The notified schedule.** The DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in November 2025 and phase in over 18 months. Board provisions apply immediately. Consent-manager provisions apply after 12 months. The remaining substantive obligations apply after 18 months.
   - Sources: Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas, https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/, pub 2025-11-14; Khurana & Khurana / search-snippet aggregators, https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/update-meity-notifies-rules-operationalising-the-dpdp-framework-in-india (only the snippet was seen). PIB backgrounder PDF, https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf, pub 2025-11-17, verbatim text: "The Rules introduce an eighteen-month period for phased compliance."
   - Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: YES (PIB + SAM).
   - Round-1 hypothesis: **CONFIRMED**.

2. **Which day the phases start.** Shardul Amarchand's table puts the milestones on 14 Nov 2025, 14 Nov 2026 and 14 May 2027. Khurana, S.S. Rana and the aggregators give 13 Nov 2025, 13 Nov 2026 and 13 May 2027.
   - This is a one-day conflict. It turns on the Gazette date (13 Nov) versus the date of the PIB release (14 Nov). See Leads.
   - Sources: SAM (above); S.S. Rana, https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/, pub 2026-02-13.
   - Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: CONFLICTING.

3. **What starts in each phase, per Shardul Amarchand.**
   - 14 Nov 2026: consent-manager registration, plus "DPB penalty powers".
   - 14 May 2027: notice and consent, breach reporting, security safeguards, children's verifiable consent, SDF obligations, and data-principal rights.
   - Source: SAM (above). Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE.

### Q2 — Purchased, third-party and scraped lead lists; publicly available data; B2B contacts

8. **What the exclusion says.** Section 3(c)(ii) excludes only personal data made (or caused to be made) publicly available (a) by the data principal themselves, or (b) by a person under a legal obligation in India to publish it.
   - Sources: IAPP (Ayush Sahay, Tsaaro), https://iapp.org/news/a/scraping-public-data-in-india-innovation-enabler-or-privacy-threat-, pub 2025-10-30; dpdpa.com and ApniLaw bare-act pages (only the snippets were seen).
   - Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: high. Class: definition. Two-source: YES.

9. **Government position on scraping.** In August 2024 the Minister of State for Electronics and IT said scraping public user data is covered by the IT Act, the IT Rules and the DPDPA, "which requires intermediaries to obtain consent". IAPP notes that "the statute and the government's stance simply do not align."
   - Source: IAPP (above). Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (ministerial statement, not binding). Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE. The search summary attributes a similar point to parliamentary responses, source unclear.

10. **Most scraped or enriched lead data probably falls outside the exclusion.** Inferred emails, phone numbers assembled from fragments, enrichment data and profiles compiled from other people's posts were not made public by the principal, so the Act applies to them in full.
    - Source: search-result synthesis attributed to law.asia / ConsentOS / India Briefing, https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/ (not fetched).
    - Confidence: medium-low. Class: definition (analysis). Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE (analysis, not a regulator).

11. **Business contact details are covered.** A corporate email, direct line or job title combined with a company name identifies an employee, who is then a data principal. The Act has no B2B carve-out.
    - Source: ComplyDP, https://www.complydp.com/articles/does-dpdp-apply-to-b2b-customer-data (vendor blog, only the snippet was seen).
    - Confidence: medium-low. Class: definition. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE (vendor). No tier-1 law-firm source was found.

12. **Liability for outsourced callers.** A business "cannot necessarily avoid responsibility merely by stating that the call was made by an external agency." Once a number has passed down a chain of processors and marketers, it may be hard to show when and how the original principal consented. Also, "consent to process a telephone number should not automatically be equated with consent to receive marketing calls."
    - Source: AMLEGALS, https://amlegals.com/telemarketing-consent-in-india-what-the-dpdp-act-means-for-businesses/, pub 2026-08-12.
    - Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (analysis). Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE (a consent.in vendor blog makes similar points; not fetched).

13. **Purchased lists and legitimate use.** No source found says a purchased lead list can be processed under a s.7 legitimate use. Everything found points to consent as the only basis for cold outreach.
    - Round-1 "no s.7 legitimate use covers purchased lists": **STILL SINGLE-SOURCE** (supported by AMLEGALS, but not stated explicitly). The s.7(a) reading for following up an inbound enquiry was **not tested** this round.

### Q3 — Consent obtained on a voice call

14. **No official guidance found.** Neither MeitY/PIB nor any tier-1 firm source found says whether oral or recorded verbal consent on a call, or IVR consent, satisfies s.6(1) "clear affirmative action", or how the Rule 3 notice can be "presented" on a call.
    - Sources searched: PIB backgrounder, KS&K, AMLEGALS. Confidence: n/a. Class: regulatory gap.

15. **Burden of proof on the fiduciary.** Under s.6(10) the fiduciary must prove valid consent was obtained. Analysts advise keeping the notice format, the user's action, timestamps, and the link between consent and each later use of the data.
    - Source: King Stubb & Kasiva (Aniket Ghosh), https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-act-consent-rules/, updated 2026-07-27.
    - Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: medium-high (s.6(10) is statutory). Class: regulatory. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE for the record-keeping advice.

16. **Separate notice required.** Per PIB, every fiduciary must issue a separate consent notice that is clear and easy to understand and explains the specific purpose.
    - Source: PIB backgrounder PDF (verbatim, extracted). Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: SINGLE (primary; the PIB press-release page makes the same point in a snippet).
    - The Rule 3 wording itself ("independently", "itemised", link) was **not re-verified** from the Gazette text this round.

17. **Vendor views on call consent.** Vendor blogs say that silence or staying on the line is not consent, and that a recorded notice alone is not consent.
    - Source: vendor blogs (relinns.com, ispectratechnologies.com) via search summary only.
    - Confidence: low. Class: definition. Two-source: NO (vendor, unverified). Do not rely on this.

### Q4 — s.8(7) erasure vs Rule 8(3) one-year retention

18. **Rule 8(3) applies to every fiduciary.** It requires all data fiduciaries, not only the Third Schedule classes, to keep personal data, associated traffic data and processing logs for at least one year from processing. SAM calls this an addition "not present under the Draft Rules".
    - Source: SAM (above; quoted in a paraphrase from the fetch summary). Accessed 2026-09-25. Confidence: medium-high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE.
    - The PIB PDF summary asserted the same, but PDF extraction did not confirm it, so it is not counted.
    - Round-1 "tension s.8(7) vs Rule 8(3)": **CONFIRMED that Rule 8(3) is universal (single-source)**.

19. **How the rules reconcile the two.** Erasure is required once the specified purpose is no longer served, _except where retention is required by law_. The Third Schedule adds inactivity-based erasure periods only for large e-commerce, social-media and gaming entities.
    - Source: SAM (above). Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE.
    - Inference, not a sourced claim: Rule 8(3) works as the "required by law" carve-out, so the one-year floor applies before s.8(7) erasure.

20. **Retention was also in the SDF proposal.** The January 2026 proposal would have made Rule 8(3) retention apply within 90 days, or "within three months of any gazette notification".
    - Sources: S.S. Rana (above); search summary for the KNN / Mondaq results.
    - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (proposal). Two-source: YES (weak; one is a summary).

## Glossary

- **SDF (Significant Data Fiduciary):** a fiduciary the Central Government notifies under s.10 on criteria such as volume and sensitivity of data. SDFs carry extra duties: a DPO in India, DPIAs, audits, and per Rules 13(4) and 15, cross-border restrictions.
- **Rule 8(3):** the minimum one-year retention of personal data, traffic data and processing logs. It applies to all fiduciaries (per SAM).
- **Third Schedule:** lists the classes (large e-commerce, social media, online gaming) with fixed inactivity-based erasure periods under Rule 8(1).
- **s.3(c)(ii):** excludes from the Act personal data made public by the data principal, or by someone legally required to publish it.
- **s.6(10):** puts the burden of proving consent on the data fiduciary.
- **Consent manager:** an India-based registered entity. Registration opens 12 months after notification (Nov 2026).

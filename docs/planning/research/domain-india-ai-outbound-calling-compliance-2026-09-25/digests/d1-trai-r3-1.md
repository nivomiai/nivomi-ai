1. The Third Amendment is "Regulations 05 of 2026", Notification No. RG-25/(25)/2023-QoS dated 18 Sep 2026, headed "TO BE PUBLISHED IN THE GAZETTE OF INDIA, EXTRAORDINARY, PART III, SECTION 4" and signed by Secretary Harinder Kumar. — S1 (TRAI, 2026-09-18); S2 lists it with release date 18/09/2026. 2. Gazette status: no evidence of eGazette publication was found as of 2026-09-25; MediaNama states "These regulations have not yet been notified." — S3 (2026-09-19); S1 header says "to be published". Confidence medium (absence of evidence; eGazette not directly checked). Class: status fact. Status: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE (only S3 states it explicitly).
3. Commencement (reg 1(3)): the regulations come into force 30 days after publication in the Official Gazette, except regs 4, 6, 8(a) and 18, which take effect after 60 days, and the Annexure items, which take effect after 90 days. All periods run from Gazette publication, so no provision is in force yet if Claim 2 holds. — S1; S3 gives the same 30/60/90 split. Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory. Status: CONFIRMED.
   - Mapping (S1 text + EM para 1): reg 4 = A2P declaration under substituted principal reg 4; reg 6 = VNO digital interface; reg 8(a) = header/content-template misuse regime; reg 18 = A2P termination charge (new reg 35A); all at **+60 days**. Annexure (+90 days) = consumer appeal (reg 23(1)(c)), AI-flag and complaint corroboration in reg 25, and the 5-template wrong-category suspension. Everything else, **including the new "Relationship"/7-day inquiry definition and the legacy-consent definition, takes effect at +30 days** (S1). S3 instead places the "inquiry-based contact window" at 60 days. That conflicts with S1's clause list, and S1 governs.
4. A2P definition, inserted as principal reg 2(ea): "'Application-to-Person call or A2P call' refers to a voice call that is initiated by an application, software system, or automated platform without direct human dialling and delivered to an individual telecom subscriber; including using autodialling, robo-calls and/or pre-recorded/artificial voice technologies". — S1; S4 quotes the same wording. Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory. Status: CONFIRMED.
5. Declaration duty, which substitutes principal reg 4 ("Intimation regarding use of A2P calls"): "Every Sender shall declare to the Originating Access Provider (OAP), in advance, about the use of Application-to-Person (A2P) calls, along with the details such as Calling Line Identification (CLI) ranges to be used for making A2P calls, or any other details as specified by the Authority…". The OAP must record the declaration on the DLT platform together with the numbering resources assigned to that Sender. — S1; S4 ("must pre-declare such use to its TSP along with the details of CLIs"). Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory. Status: CONFIRMED.
6. Undeclared A2P calls, under the proviso to reg 4: "any such call made by a Sender without prior declaration to the OAP, shall be treated as Unsolicited commercial communication, and the OAP shall take action against such Sender". — S1; MediaNama round-2 coverage says the same. Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED.
7. The EM says declaration applies to **all** A2P calls, not only commercial ones, to prevent "misuse of A2P calling for sending UCC in the garb of transaction or service calling". It also notes that networks cannot tell "a pre-recorded or artificially generated human voice" from a human caller, which is why pre-declaration is required. For an AI-voice platform this means every call, including service or transactional calls, must come from declared CLI ranges. — S1 EM §A2P. Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory (EM interpretation). Status: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE for the "all A2P calls" scope.
8. Charge, new principal reg 35A: "The Terminating Access Provider (TAP) may charge the Originating Access Provider (OAP) upto Rs. 0.05 (five paisa only) per minute on per minute pulse basis for A2P calls". The charge is capped at the TAP's lowest tariff to senders. It does not apply to A2P calls made using the 140xx, 1600xx, 1601xx or other designated commercial series, or to calls made by or for TRAI. — S1; S4 ("up to ₹0.05 per minute… levied by the Terminating Access Provider"); S3 ("5 paise a minute"). Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory. Status: CONFIRMED. (The series exemption is from S1 only.)
9. Inquiry window: the substituted definition 2(bb) "Relationship" (ii) reads "On the basis of an inquiry for goods or products or services, submitted by the Recipient to Sender within seven days immediately preceding the date of the receiving of communication, which relationship has not been previously terminated by either party: Provided that such an inquiry shall be made in writing or through digital means, and shall be maintained in a verifiable form by the Sender". — S1; S4 ("Commercial communications may be sent … on the basis of an inquiry … only for a period of seven days from the date of such inquiry … made in writing or through digital means … maintained in a verifiable form"); S3 ("limited to a seven-day window"). Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory. Status: CONFIRMED.
10. The same definition adds 2(bb)(iii): a relationship also exists on the basis of an **application** for products or services submitted within the **three months** before the communication. The EM explains that an "inquiry" means the customer "has only shown the interest", while an "application" means the customer "has already taken a decision and applied". The old "social reasons" limb (iv) is removed. — S1 (text + EM 4.2 v, viii). Confidence high. Status: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE (no independent publisher seen for the 3-month application limb in this round).
11. How the inquiry must be evidenced: an oral inquiry does not qualify. The inquiry must be in writing or by digital means and kept by the Sender "in a verifiable form". The EM notes that TRAI saw Senders "misinterpret 'inquiry' to mean 'oral inquiry'". In a complaint, the OAP verifies the "existence of consent" with the Sender, and it must "appear genuine to an objective, reasonable observer". The text has **no** requirement to register the inquiry on the DLT/CRF. — S1 (2(bb) proviso; EM 4.2 iii; EM on reg 2(y) ii). Confidence high. Status: evidence rule CONFIRMED via S4; the finding of no registration duty for inquiries is STILL SINGLE-SOURCE (absence in S1).
12. The EM says "Relationship" is defined "for the purpose of inferred consent" (2(ah)). The text **does not** say which category (promotional, service or transactional) or which number series applies to inquiry-based calls. It also does not say whether the relationship overrides a customer's registered NCPR/DND preference. The EM's own phrase is that "commercial communication based on such relationship should be permitted for a limited period of maximum seven days". — S1 (EM 4.2 i, iii). Confidence medium. Class: primary-regulatory with an interpretive gap. Status: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE.
13. Legacy consent: the substituted 2(y) "Explicit Consent" covers consent that is either verified directly from the Recipient and recorded by the Consent Registrar, **or** "obtained by the Sender through any verifiable means prior to or outside the Consent Registration Function framework, and subsequently registered in the Consent Register in accordance with the procedure specified by the Authority". The EM says customers get an SMS when a legacy consent is uploaded to the CRF, with a link to manage or revoke it (piloted with 11 banks under a 10.12.2025 Direction). — S1; S4 ("valid only where … obtained through verifiable means and … subsequently registered on the digital platform of the TSPs"); S3. Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED.
14. The new definition of "consent" (2(k)) is limited to permission for the communication channel. Explanation 3 says nothing in TCCCPR exempts a Sender from the DPDP Act, 2023, and the EM says a TCCCPR revocation stops only commercial communications and does not revoke DPDP consent. — S1. Confidence high. Status: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE.
15. Service Call, substituted 2(bh): sub-clause (i) covers calls to the Sender's own customer about a product or service they have procured (warranty, delivery, OTPs and similar) and needs no explicit consent. Sub-clause (ii) covers calls "to facilitate a commercial transaction involving the ongoing purchase or the use … after obtaining Explicit Consent". That consent is "valid for a period of seven days or until revoked … whichever is earlier, and it may be renewed at the instance of the customer for an additional period of seven days at a time". — S1; S5 (2025 regime: "validity shall be limited to a maximum of 7 days"). Confidence high. Status: **7-day validity CONFIRMED**; the renewal-by-customer mechanism is new in 2026 and STILL SINGLE-SOURCE.
16. Number series (EM, citing existing mandates): "promotional calls can be made by Senders using number resources from 140xx series only". 1600xx is for service and transactional calls by BFSI entities and government. 1601xx is "for Service and Transactional Voice Calls by entities in sectors other than BFSI and government". Calls from 140xx reach a customer "only when the customer has not registered the preference to block such calls". — S1 EM; S5 (140 for promotional, 160-series for service/transactional). Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED (1601xx split: single-source S1).
17. Call-management apps, substituted reg 34A: no call-management app may tag, block or filter calls from designated commercial or government series, or blanket-tag them as spam. User-level blocking on the user's own device is allowed. Spam reporting in apps must be routed to the DLT platform. — S1; S3; S4 (140xx/1600xx/1601xx must not be flagged as suspected spam). Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED.
18. AI/ML flagging, new reg 21A: the TAP's AI/ML UCC_Detect system flags "Suspected high probability UCC CLI" and shares it with the OAP within 2 hours. The OAP notifies the Sender. If five or more of a Sender's CLIs are flagged within 10 days, the steps escalate: (1) KYC re-verification; (2) physical KYC, and on mismatch or misuse, a 15-day bar on outgoing services of all the Sender's resources; (3) further instances go to the reg 25(6)(b) sanctions. Series 140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx are never flagged, and Senders get a route to challenge a flag. — S1; S3 (reg 21A, 2 hours). Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED (core); escalation detail single-source.
19. Complaint thresholds, substituted reg 25(5)(d): the OAP must immediately suspend the Sender's outgoing resources used for UCC if either (a) there are ≥3 complaints from unique recipients in 10 days across all operators **and** one of the Sender's CLIs is AI-flagged, or (b) there are ≥5 complaints from unique recipients in 10 days. An investigation follows, with 5 business days for the Sender to respond. — S1; press headline "3 complaints can trigger action" (indianmasterminds, search snippet only). Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED (weak second source). This clause is not in the Annexure, so it applies at +30 days. The +90-day Annexure item is the corroboration step in the main clause of reg 25.
20. Sanctions on Senders, reg 25(6): a first violation bars outgoing services of "all telecom resources including PRI/SIP trunks, SIMs etc. allotted to the Sender … for a period of fifteen days". Subsequent violations mean all resources are "disconnected by all the Access Providers for one year", the Sender is blacklisted for that year with no new resources, and the devices used are blocked for a year. One barred number may be kept. — S1 (2026 re-enactment); S5 ("First offence: 15-day bar …; subsequent offences: 1-year disconnection and blacklisting"). Confidence high. Class: primary-regulatory. Status: **CONFIRMED**.
21. Restoration charge, substituted reg 29: TRAI may order resources restored on payment of Rs 5,000 per resource, capped at Rs 5 lakh. Each DID on a PRI or SIP trunk counts as a separate resource, and a partial restoration costs at least half the full charge. A Sender may represent to TRAI within 60 days of the action. — S1. Confidence high. Status: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE.
22. Telemarketer liability, reg 22(1)(a): if a telemarketer misused headers or templates, or helped do so, all its resources are disconnected for one year and it is blacklisted. — S1; S4. Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED. (Relevant if the platform is classed as a Telemarketer or aggregator.)
23. 90-day wait: a Sender may not seek consent again for 90 days after a customer opts out or revokes, unless the customer opts in sooner. This comes from the 2025 Second Amendment; the 2026 Third Amendment neither restates nor alters it (no "ninety days" consent rule appears in S1). — S5 ("No consent requests may be sent for 90 days after a customer opts out (unless the customer opts in sooner)"); S6/TRAI per round 2. Confidence high. Status: **CONFIRMED**.
24. Rationale for the inquiry window: the March 2026 draft proposed deleting "inquiry" from "Relationship" entirely. TRAI restored it in the final text after stakeholder pushback, but cut the period to 7 days (from 3 months) and required written or digital evidence. — S1 EM 4.2 ii–iii; S4 (draft dated 13 March 2026). Confidence high. Status: CONFIRMED (draft-to-final change shown by S1 alone).

### Answer to Q2: does the inquiry window change the round-2 "prospect = promotional" conclusion?

The round-2 conclusion is refined, not overturned. What is evidenced (S1, S5):

- A web-form submission is a written or digital inquiry. It creates a "Relationship" under 2(bb)(ii) that lasts **7 days from the submission**, provided the Sender keeps it in verifiable form (timestamp, form payload, source URL, the number the lead entered). After 7 days, calls need registered Explicit Consent in the CRF, or must be made as ordinary promotional calls from 140xx subject to preference scrubbing. A web **application** for a product (for example a loan or account application) extends the window to 3 months under 2(bb)(iii).
- The text does not say that an inquiry changes the call's **category or number series**, or that it overrides an NCPR/DND block (Claim 12). The existing mandates say promotional calls go **only** from 140xx, reach a customer only if they have not blocked that preference, and that 1600xx/1601xx are for service and transactional calls (Claim 16). A sales or lead-qualification call to a lead who has not bought anything is promotional in content. No retrieved source says an inquiry turns that call into service or transactional.
- The only non-promotional route for a prospect is Service Call 2(bh)(ii): a call to "facilitate a commercial transaction involving the ongoing purchase" after **Explicit Consent** (valid 7 days, renewable by the customer). That fits appointment scheduling for a purchase already under way, from 1601xx (1600xx for BFSI), with the consent recorded.
- Recommended engine rule (conservative; inference, not a stated rule):
  - Sales or qualification calls to a web-form lead go from **140xx**.
  - Allow them only if (a) the lead has not blocked promotional calls, or (b) there is registered Explicit Consent covering promotions, **and** (c) the call falls within 7 days of the evidenced inquiry, or 3 months of an application, where the inquiry is the only basis.
  - Treat the inquiry as a time-limited inferred-consent basis, never as an NCPR override, until TRAI clarifies.
  - Every call, in any series, must come from declared A2P CLI ranges (Claims 5–7).

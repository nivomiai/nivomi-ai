## Claims

### A. Numbering (Q1)

1. Amended regulation 3(1) requires every access provider to ensure commercial communication on its network "takes place only using registered headers or the number resources allotted to the Senders from special series assigned for the purpose of commercial communication". Source S1 (TRAI, 12 Feb 2025). Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary verbatim; S2 snippet corroborates the series mandate generally. → Round-1 hypothesis "regular 10-digit numbers may not be used for commercial calls": **CONFIRMED** (S1 regulation 3 + Explanatory Memorandum para 16 and para 21, which describe UTMs using "normal 10-digit mobile/landline numbers"; S2 corroborates).
2. Amended regulation 3(2) says an unregistered sender must not make any commercial communication, and all of its telecom resources may be suspended or disconnected. Source S1. Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary (verbatim).
3. The Explanatory Memorandum (para 18) says promotional auto-dialer/robo calls "should be permitted through 140-series numbers only". It also says service and transactional robo calls "should be permitted through 1600 or any other series allotted for the purpose". Source S1. Confidence: high (it is the regulator's own text, but it is memorandum reasoning, not an operative clause). Class: regulatory. Two-source: S2 snippet ("Promotional calls must use the 140 series, while transactional and service calls must use the newly allocated 1600 series"). → Round-1 "promotional = 140, service/transactional = 1600": **CONFIRMED**, but see claim 4.
4. The "or any other series allotted for the purpose" wording in S1 means the regulation does not make 1600 the only permitted series for non-BFSI service/transactional calls. The operative clauses use the neutral phrase "special series assigned for the purpose of making service and transactional calls" (sender agreement clause in amended reg. 22). Source S1. Confidence: medium (this is a textual reading, not official guidance). Class: regulatory. Two-source: single-source (my reading of the primary). → Round-1 hypothesis about the "160 vs 1600" naming: **STILL SINGLE-SOURCE / UNRESOLVED**. The 2025 regulation uses only "1600". No source retrieved this run explains the Aug 2024 "160" wording.
5. Regulation 4 (substituted) says every sender must notify its originating access provider in writing, in advance, when it uses auto dialers or robo-calls, and must state the intended objective of those calls. Source S1. Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary (verbatim). **Directly relevant to AI voice agents.**
6. New regulation 34A bars call-management apps from blanket-blocking or spam-tagging calls from the designated number series. Source S1. Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary.
7. TRAI issued a Direction on 19 Nov 2025 (press release 135/2025) requiring BFSI entities regulated by RBI, SEBI and PFRDA to adopt the 1600 series in phases. Sources S3 (TRAI) and S4 (Legitpro/Mondaq). Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: **YES**. → Round-1 "Nov 2025 direction mandated 1600 for BFSI": **CONFIRMED** (it covers RBI/SEBI/PFRDA entities; IRDAI came separately).
8. The RBI-entity deadlines were: scheduled commercial banks by 1 Jan 2026; large NBFCs, payments banks and small finance banks by 1 Feb 2026; remaining NBFCs, co-operative banks and RRBs by 1 Mar 2026. For SEBI entities (mutual funds/AMCs, qualified stockbrokers) the dates were about 15 Feb and 15 Mar 2026. For PFRDA entities (CRAs, pension fund managers) the date was 15 Feb 2026. Source S4, with the RBI dates also in the search-engine synthesis of S3 and PIB. Confidence: medium-high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: RBI dates yes (S4 + S3 summary); SEBI/PFRDA dates single-source (S4).
9. A separate TRAI direction (reported 17 Dec 2025) requires IRDAI-regulated entities to adopt 1600 for service and transactional calls by 15 Feb 2026. Reports list insurers, brokers, agents, TPAs and insurtechs, but that list comes from secondary outlets. Sources S5 (Business Standard) plus Upstox/Lokmat snippets. Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: nominally yes, but the outlets likely recycle one wire story. The primary TRAI direction was not read.
10. DoT has reserved the 1600 series for BFSI entities and certain government organisations. Source S4, echoed by the search synthesis of the TRAI/PIB release. Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: weak (S4 + an aggregator). The DoT allotment order itself was not found.
11. The 1600 mandate covers service and transactional calls only. Promotional/marketing calls by BFSI entities stay outside it and fall under consent rules and the 140 series. Sources S4 and S5. Confidence: medium-high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: yes.
12. No evidence was retrieved of a TRAI/DoT direction extending mandatory 1600 adoption beyond RBI/SEBI/PFRDA/IRDAI-regulated entities (and government) by 2026-09-25. Confidence: low. This is an absence of evidence from only two targeted searches. Class: regulatory. Two-source: n/a. A non-BFSI business making service calls must use a "special series assigned" for service/transactional calls (S1). Whether that means 1600 for everyone is not confirmed (see claim 4).

### B. DLT / telemarketer chain (Q2)

13. Access providers must keep commercial communication traceable, with at most two telemarketers in the chain: "one Telemarketer with Aggregator Function and one Telemarketer with Delivery Function". Registered telemarketers (RTMs) must use digital platforms that record the trace. Source S1 (amended reg. 22(1)(e)). Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary (verbatim). **Implication:** a platform placing calls for a business sits in this chain and would need RTM (aggregator or delivery) status. That is an inference, not stated for voice-AI platforms specifically.
14. Access providers must sign agreements with registered senders. The agreements make the sender solely responsible for using only registered headers or its allotted numbers from the service/transactional special series, and for not mixing in promotional content. Senders must self-certify their registration details, headers, content templates and consent templates every year, or those are automatically suspended. Senders get a right of legal action against a telemarketer that misuses their headers or templates. Source S1 (amended reg. 22(1)(i)). Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary.
15. Principal Entity registration on DLT is done through an access provider and is valid for 5 years. The TSP assigns 140-series numbers after verifying documents. Promotional voice templates must be registered on DLT. PE–telemarketer binding (disclosure of the whole chain) is required under recent TRAI directions. Source: a search-engine synthesis of vendor pages (frejun.com, go2market.in, fast2sms, vobiz). Confidence: low. Class: regulatory. Two-source: **SINGLE-SOURCE / vendor-grade**. Needs confirmation from a primary operator or TRAI document.

### C. Categorisation (Q3)

16. The new definition says a "Promotional Voice Call" is any voice commercial communication containing promotional material or advertisement, and "if promotional content is mixed with any type of commercial Voice Call, such voice call shall be treated as a Promotional Voice Call". Source S1 (clause 2(av)). Confidence: high. Class: definition. Two-source: **YES** (S1 + S2 snippet). → Round-1 "any promotional content makes a call promotional": **CONFIRMED**.
17. A "Service Voice Call" is one of two things. (i) A call to the sender's own _customer or subscriber_ about a product or service already used or bought (warranty, recall, delivery, balance alerts, etc.), not promotional, needing no explicit consent. (ii) A call to a recipient "to facilitate or complete a commercial transaction involving the ongoing purchase" after explicit consent, where that consent is valid for **seven days**. Source S1 (clause 2(bh)). Confidence: high. Class: definition. Two-source: YES (S1 + S2 snippet).
18. A "Transactional Voice Call" is a call to the sender's customer "in response to Customer initiated transaction within thirty minutes of the transaction" (OTPs, transaction alerts, refunds). Anything sent after that window counts as service. Source S1 (clause 2(bt) + EM para 9). Confidence: high. Class: definition. Two-source: single primary for the 30-minute rule.
19. Explanatory Memorandum para 11 and para 40 say communications to _prospective customers_ ("individuals who have shown interest in a product or service but have not yet made a purchase") "should be included under the definition of promotional messages only". Source S1. Confidence: high (regulator's own reasoning). Class: definition. Two-source: single primary. → Round-1 "unsettled whether a callback to an inbound lead is promotional or service": **LARGELY OVERTURNED**. By TRAI's own memorandum, a follow-up or lead-qualification call to someone who enquired but has not bought is promotional: 140 series, preference/consent rules apply, but explicit digital consent (DCA) lets it reach DND-registered recipients. A narrow exception may exist under 2(bh)(ii): a non-promotional call that facilitates an "ongoing purchase" within 7 days of explicit consent. How far that reaches for lead qualification is untested.
20. An appointment-scheduling call to an _existing customer_ about a service they already use plausibly fits Service 2(bh)(i). For a _prospect_ it falls under para 40 (promotional), unless it only facilitates an ongoing purchase under explicit 7-day consent. Source S1 (textual reading). Confidence: medium. Class: definition. Two-source: single-source (inference). No TRAI FAQ or law-firm guidance on appointment calls was found.
21. Inferred consent lasts for the duration of the contract. For messages it must be "clearly and reasonably inferred from the registered Content Template". Source S1 (clause 2(ah) provisos; EM para 14). Confidence: high. Class: definition. Two-source: single primary.
22. Customers cannot block transactional calls, inferred-consent service calls or government calls. Only explicit-consent service communication can be blocked via preferences. Source S1 (amended "Fully blocked" clause (z); EM para 13). Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: single primary.

### D. Enforcement (Q4 and regulation)

23. The escalation ladder for a registered sender who misuses the 1600 or service series is in reg. 25. On a first violation, outgoing services on **all** the sender's telecom resources (including PRI/SIP trunks) are barred. On repeat violations, all resources across all access providers are disconnected for up to 1 year and the sender is blacklisted. Each DID on a PRI/SIP trunk counts as a separate resource. Source S1. Confidence: high (text read; exact sub-thresholds not transcribed here). Class: enforcement. Two-source: single primary.
24. Customers must complain within 7 days (previously 3). Action against unregistered senders needs at least 10 complaints within the last 7 days. Source S1 (reg. 23 / EM), plus S6 (PIB) snippet, which also says the access-provider action window for unregistered senders dropped from 30 to 5 days. Confidence: high. Class: enforcement. Two-source: YES for 7 days (S1 + S6); the 5-day window is S6 only.
25. On 19 Mar 2025 the Government said telcos had blacklisted **1,150** entities/individuals and disconnected **over 18.8 lakh** telecom resources. UTM complaints fell from 1,89,419 (Aug 2024) to 1,34,821 (Jan 2025). Access providers face fines up to Rs 10 lakh for non-implementation. Sources S7 (IANS) and S8 (MediaNama), both apparently reporting PIB 2112743 (S6). Confidence: medium-high. Class: quantitative/enforcement. Two-source: YES (two outlets + PIB title match, though PIB body not read). → Round-1 enforcement figures: **CONFIRMED** (date 19 Mar 2025; attributed to PIB PRID 2112743).
26. For Q1 FY2026-27 (Apr–Jun 2026), news reports say TRAI recorded 1.085 million UCC complaints (0.553 million actionable). 1,37,053 resources were barred for 15 days (first violation). Action covered 1.83 lakh resources overall. 2,873 SMS headers were blocked and 263 senders blacklisted for one year. Source S9 (two outlets, ~4 Aug 2026, recycling one TRAI release). Confidence: medium. Class: quantitative. Two-source: **STILL SINGLE-SOURCE** (one upstream; TRAI primary not read).

## Glossary

- **TCCCPR 2018**: TRAI's Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations. Amended by the Second Amendment of 12 Feb 2025, in force 60 days after gazette publication.
- **140-series**: Numbering series for promotional voice calls (EM para 18; S2).
- **1600-series**: Numbering series for service/transactional voice calls. Mandatory for RBI/SEBI/PFRDA/IRDAI-regulated entities under the Nov–Dec 2025 directions. Reportedly reserved by DoT for BFSI and government.
- **Special series**: The regulation's neutral term for DoT/TRAI-designated commercial number ranges.
- **Sender / Principal Entity (PE)**: The business on whose behalf commercial communication is sent. It registers on the access provider's DLT.
- **RTM**: Registered Telemarketer. It is either a _Telemarketer with Aggregator Function_ or a _Telemarketer with Delivery Function_; at most one of each is allowed in a chain.
- **UTM**: Unregistered telemarketer, i.e. a commercial caller using ordinary 10-digit numbers.
- **OAP / TAP**: Originating / Terminating Access Provider.
- **Inferred consent**: Consent reasonably inferred from the customer relationship. Valid for the duration of the contract.
- **Explicit (digital) consent**: Consent recorded via the DCA platform. It lets promotional calls reach DND-registered recipients. For service calls under 2(bh)(ii) it lasts 7 days.
- **DCA**: Digital Consent Acquisition platform run by access providers.
- **Call Management Application**: Caller-ID/spam apps. Barred from blanket-blocking designated series (reg. 34A).

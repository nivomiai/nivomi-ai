## Claims

### A. CCPA 2026 draft — primary text retrieved (S1)

1. The CCPA has a draft titled "Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Pesky, Promotional and Unsolicited Commercial Communications, 2026", issued under s.18(2) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. — S1 (primary text), S2 2026-09-24, S3 c.2026-09-15; accessed 2026-09-25; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED (primary text + two independent press).
2. The retrieved text is visibly a committee working draft, not a clean notified text: it contains bracketed editorial notes such as "(need to revise)" on the Consent definition, clause 6(2) and clause 8, "(45/60/90)" alternatives for cooling-off, "180/365 days" alternatives for record retention, "(Suggestion by committee: …)" notes, and a blank escalation forum "___**(CCPA)**_" in clause 7(4). — S1; confidence HIGH (direct reading); class regulatory-draft status; STILL SINGLE (primary doc itself; press did not comment on its draft-of-draft character). Implication: every number below is unsettled.
3. Publication date and comment deadline were NOT found: S1 text carries no date or deadline; S2 states no deadline; S3 says only that the draft was published about a week before its ~15 Sep 2026 article. No PIB/DoCA notice was found. — S1, S2, S3; confidence MEDIUM; class absence; STILL SINGLE on date (approx. early Sep 2026 per S3 only).
4. Commencement: clause 1(2) says the guidelines "shall come into force on the publication by the Central Consumer Protection Authority" — i.e. no transition period is written into the draft. — S1; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; STILL SINGLE (primary only).
5. Scope (clause 3): applies to persons/entities that (a) send or cause to send Commercial Communication ("Sender"), (b) engage the Sender, (c) are the intended beneficiary, (d) in whose name the communication is made — when made through (i) normal 10-digit numbers not registered as Telemarketer or Principal Entity under TCCCPR 2018, or (ii) app-based or other "Communication Platform and Channel" services. — S1; S2 paraphrases the same four roles plus unregistered 10-digit numbers; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
6. Illustrations to clause 5 state that where Bank X engages Y to call, "These guidelines apply to both X and Y", and in a three-party chain (brand W, fintech X, caller Y) they apply to W, X and Y — i.e. a SaaS operator acting as Sender and its business customer as engager/beneficiary are both in scope. — S1; S3 ("all business entities, intermediaries, telemarketers, commercial senders, brand beneficiaries, and foreign entities"); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
7. Terms of Reference preamble also states "All intermediary communication channels, platforms and applications shall be covered, as applicable", and clause 10 Explanation extends applicability to entities located outside India where the recipient is in India. — S1; S3 (foreign entities); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
8. AI disclosure (Annexure 1 ground (k)): it is an unsolicited/unwarranted communication to initiate one "using an artificial, synthetic or AI-generated voice or text, on behalf of a brand/beneficiary, without clearly and audibly/visibly disclosing to the recipient, at the outset of the communication, that it is AI-generated"; illustration: a synthetic-voice loan pitch with no disclosure — "The Sender and the beneficiary brand are in violation". — S1 (verbatim); S2 ("The disclosure must be audible"; "at the outset"); S3 ("clear, upfront disclosures"); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
9. Consent (clause 6(1)): "No Commercial Communication shall be made to a consumer save on the basis of Explicit Consent obtained for the specific brand/beneficiary and product., or as per the consumer's registered preference in a Consent Registry." — S1; S2 ("explicit consent for each specific brand or beneficiary and product"); S3 ("explicit, verifiable digital consent" per brand); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
10. For voice calls/messages via telecom resources, Explicit Consent is valid "only if acquired through the Digital Consent Acquisition (DCA) facility of the Telecom Service Provider, in accordance with TCCCPR 2018 as amended"; for other channels an equivalent mechanism "as may be prescribed by the Authority" (flagged "need to revise"). — S1; confidence HIGH on text, LOW on finality; class regulatory-draft; STILL SINGLE (press did not report the DCA-only rule).
11. Cooling-off (clause 6(3) and Annexure ground (c)): after opt-out, the brand or its agent "shall not seek fresh consent … for a period of ninety (45/60/90) days from the date of opt-out, consistent with the cooling-off period prescribed under TCCCPR 2018, as amended." — S1; S2 (45/60/90 undecided); S3 ("up to 90 days"); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
12. Opt-out: ground (f) makes it a violation to call "without giving a clear, simple, free and effective option to opt out, and a confirmation of opt-out where the consumer avails of it"; clause 6(4) requires opt-outs to be given effect and confirmed within clause 7 timelines. — S1; S2; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
13. Identification: ground (d) makes it a violation to call "without clearly identifying the maker and the purpose of the communication" (illustration: insurance broker that does not identify itself or the purpose). — S1; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; STILL SINGLE.
14. Other grounds relevant to an outbound voice-agent product: unregistered/unauthorised Sender Identity (a); calling despite DND/Consent Registry opt-out (b); unauthorised employee/agent (e); spoofed identity (h); "persistent follow-up communications after a single enquiry, without a continuing relationship, engagement, or fresh consent" (j); use of personal data collected/processed in violation of the DPDP Act 2023 to identify, profile or target (l). — S1; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; STILL SINGLE (press summaries do not itemise these).
15. Exclusions (clause 4): transactional/service communication via TCCCPR-registered transactional headers; communication under subsisting Explicit Consent; government/statutory communications; communication necessary to perform an existing contract that does not solicit an unrelated sale; employer–employee and existing professional–client communication; anything the Authority exempts by order. — S1; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; STILL SINGLE.
16. Grievance (clause 7): designate a Nodal Grievance Officer and publish contacts; acknowledge an opt-out request or complaint within 24 hours and resolve within 7 days, unless TCCCPR 2018 sets a shorter period for telecom resources. — S1; S2; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.
17. Record-keeping (clause 8, flagged "need to revise"): maintain "verifiable records of Consent, opt-out requests and Sender Identity for a minimum period of 180/365 days as may be prescribed", producible on demand; Authority may require periodic self-certification of compliance with the guidelines and TCCCPR 2018. — S1; S3 reports "up to 365 days" (S3 misreports a minimum as a maximum); confidence HIGH on text; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED (the number is unsettled).
18. The draft contains NO duty to record calls, retain call audio or produce transcripts; its record-keeping duty covers consent, opt-out and sender identity only. — S1 (full text read); S2 ("Not explicitly addressed"); confidence HIGH; class absence; CONFIRMED.
19. Enforcement: any contravention constitutes a misleading advertisement (s.2(28)), unfair trade practice (s.2(47)) and/or violation of consumer rights (s.2(9)); CCPA may pass orders under ss.20 and 21 of the Consumer Protection Act after a hearing; CCPA may share a repeat-offender record with TRAI and MeitY for coordinated action including disconnection/suspension of the Sender Identity. — S1; S2 (ss.20, 21); S3 (unfair trade practice/misleading ad; TRAI/MeitY coordination); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED. (Penalty quantum under s.21 was not retrieved this run.)
20. Relationship to TRAI/TCCCPR and DPDP: the guidelines are "in addition to, and not in derogation of" TCCCPR 2018, the IT Intermediary Rules 2021, the DPDP Act 2023 and "DPDP Rules, 2025"; but "In the event of any conflict … the provisions of the other law shall prevail" (clause 9). Intermediary platforms remain subject to the Telecom Act/TCCCPR or IT Act as applicable. — S1; S2 (TCCCPR timelines take precedence); confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; CONFIRMED.

### C. Participant call recording

23. Supreme Court, Vibhor Garg v. Neha (judgment 14 Jul 2025, Nagarathna and S.C. Sharma JJ.) held secretly recorded spousal telephone conversations admissible in matrimonial proceedings, balancing Article 21 privacy against the right to a fair trial; commentary treats it as an evidence-law ruling (s.122 Evidence Act), not a ruling that such recording is lawful. — S6 (SC Observer, snippet); EPW 2025 and Law School Policy Review 2025-09-28 critiques (titles/snippets only); confidence MEDIUM; class case-law; CONFIRMED on holding (two publishers), prior-round characterisation upheld.
24. ### D. DPDP interplay

26. The CCPA 2026 draft refers to consent being "governed by the applicable existing legal and regulatory framework, including sectoral requirements under the DPDP Act" and makes use of DPDP-violating personal data for targeting a ground of violation (Annexure ground (l)). — S1; confidence HIGH; class regulatory-draft; STILL SINGLE.
27. Vendor blogs assert that under DPDP Rules 2025 a generic "this call may be recorded" message may not suffice and that recording needs specific, informed, purpose-stated consent with logged timestamps; no law firm or government source corroborating these specifics was found. — S8 (frejun.com, caller.digital, cleartouch.in, gistly.ai, relinns.com, via search snippets); confidence LOW; class vendor claim; STILL SINGLE-QUALITY (do not rely). Some snippet details (e.g. "48-hour advance notice before deletion", "90 days" erasure) look like conflations and were not verified.

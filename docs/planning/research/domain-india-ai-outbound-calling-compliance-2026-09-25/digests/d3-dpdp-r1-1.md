## Claims

### A. Status and commencement

1. **When the Rules were notified.** MeitY made the DPDP Rules 2025 by notification G.S.R. 846(E), dated 13 November 2025, under section 40(1)–(2) of the Act. The draft had been published as G.S.R. 02(E) on 3 January 2025.
   - Source: S1, preamble. Publisher: MeitY / Gazette of India. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by #2. S3 gives the notification date as 14 Nov 2025; see Leads, contradiction C1.

2. **Consultation.** PIB states that the Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 after a consultation that received 6,915 inputs.
   - Source: S3. Publisher: PIB (Government of India). Pub date: 2025-11-17. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory / quantitative.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by #1 (the fact of notification). The input count is single-source.

3. **Phased commencement of the Rules (Rule 1(2)–(4)):**
   - Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 take effect on the date of publication in the Gazette. These cover definitions and the Data Protection Board.
   - Rule 4 (Consent Manager registration) takes effect one year after publication.
   - Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 take effect eighteen months after publication. This group includes notice, security, breach, retention, children's data and rights.
   - Source: S1, Rule 1. Publisher: MeitY. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by #4 (S4) and S3 ("eighteen-month period for phased compliance").

4. **Matching dates for the Act.** A law firm reports three commencement dates for provisions of both the Act and the Rules:
   - 14 Nov 2025: Board establishment, commencement provisions, and amendments to the RTI and TRAI Acts.
   - 14 Nov 2026: Consent Manager registration, and the Board's penalty powers over it.
   - 14 May 2027: the core provisions. These are notice and consent, breach reporting, security safeguards, children's consent, Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF) obligations, data principal rights, cross-border transfer and the Board's powers.
   - Source: S4. Publisher: Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas. Pub date: 2025-11-21. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: medium. The S.O. number of the Act commencement notification was not retrieved.
   - Class: regulatory.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by #3 for the Rules side. The mapping of Act sections to dates is single-source (S4).

5. **What is in force on 2026-09-25.** On the notified schedule, only the Board and definitional provisions are in force. The Consent Manager regime starts in Nov 2026. The fiduciary's core obligations do not start until May 2027: notice (s.5 / R.3), consent (s.6), security (R.6), breach (R.7), retention (R.8), children (s.9 / R.10), and rights and grievances (ss.11–14 / R.14).
   - Source: S1 R.1 and S4. Pub dates: 2025-11-13 and 2025-11-21. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: medium. A later amendment could not be ruled out (see #6 and Leads C2).
   - Class: regulatory.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by #3 and #4.

6. **Proposed acceleration (not confirmed).** At a stakeholder meeting on 23 January 2026, MeitY proposed:
   - shortening the 18-month window to 12 months for Significant Data Fiduciaries, which would move the deadline from 13 May 2027 to 13 Nov 2026;
   - bringing SDF notification, Rule 13(4), Rule 15 (cross-border transfer) and the s.17(2) exemptions into force immediately;
   - requiring data fiduciaries to keep data for at least one year from the last date of processing, within 90 days of the amendment.

   Comments were due by 4 Feb 2026. As of 13 Feb 2026 no amendment had been notified.
   - Source: S5. Publisher: S.S. Rana & Co. Pub date: 2026-02-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: medium on the fact of the proposal; low on its current status.
   - Class: regulatory (pending).
   - Two-source status: single-source as read. Search snippets from GoTrust, CADP, Mondaq and Chambers carry the same headline but were not read.

### B. Roles: fiduciary vs processor

7. **Definitions.** A "Data Fiduciary" is any person who, alone or with others, determines the purpose and means of processing personal data. A "Data Processor" is any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.
   - Source: S2, s.2(i) and s.2(k). Publisher: Government of India. Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: high. Class: definition.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("Key Terms").

8. **Fiduciary stays responsible.** The fiduciary is responsible for compliance "irrespective of any agreement to the contrary", including for processing done on its behalf by a processor (s.8(1)). It may engage a processor only under a valid contract (s.8(2)).
   - Source: S2, s.8(1)–(2). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by #9 (Rule 6(1)(f) builds on it) and S4.

9. **Security safeguards cover processors.** Reasonable security safeguards must cover processing done by a processor. The fiduciary–processor contract must contain appropriate provisions on security safeguards.
   - Source: S1, Rule 6(1) and 6(1)(f). Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
   - Two-source status: confirmed by S4 ("appropriate language in contracts" with processors).

10. **Withdrawal and erasure flow down to processors.** When consent is withdrawn, the fiduciary must cease processing and cause its processors to cease (s.6(6)). It must also cause its processors to erase data it made available to them (s.8(7)(b)).
    - Source: S2, s.6(6) and s.8(7)(b). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: single-source (statute text).

11. **Inference: the SaaS platform is ordinarily a processor.** This is an inference from #7, not a source statement. A platform that calls, records and summarises leads for business customers who decide whom to call and why is ordinarily a Data Processor, and each business customer is the Data Fiduciary. The platform would become a fiduciary for any purpose it decides itself, such as using lead data or transcripts to improve its own models.
    - Source: derived from S2 s.2(i) and s.2(k).
    - Confidence: medium. Class: definition.
    - Two-source status: single-source. No law-firm analysis of AI-calling or SaaS roles was read.

### C. Notice

12. **Notice with every consent request.** Every consent request must be accompanied or preceded by a notice. The notice must state:
    - the personal data and the purpose of processing;
    - how to withdraw consent and exercise rights under s.13;
    - how to complain to the Board.

    For consent given before commencement, a notice must be given "as soon as it is reasonably practicable", and processing may continue until consent is withdrawn (s.5(2)).
    - Source: S2, s.5(1)–(2). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by #13 (Rule 3) and S3.

13. **Content of the notice (Rule 3).** The notice must:
    - be understandable on its own, independently of any other information;
    - be in clear and plain language;
    - contain at minimum an itemised description of the personal data, and the specified purpose(s) with a specific description of the goods, services or uses;
    - give a link to the fiduciary's website or app, and describe other means to withdraw consent (as easily as it was given), exercise rights, and complain to the Board.
    - Source: S1, Rule 3(a)–(c). Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("separate consent notice ... specific purpose").

14. **Languages.** The Data Principal must be given the option to read the notice (s.5(3)), and any consent request (s.6(3)), in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution. The consent request must also give the contact details of the DPO or another authorised person.
    - Source: S2, s.5(3) and s.6(3). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: single-source (statute text; not repeated in S1, S3 or S4 as read).

### D. Consent, legitimate uses and lead lists

15. **Standard of consent.** Consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action". It must be limited to the data necessary for the specified purpose. Any part of a consent that infringes the Act is invalid to that extent.
    - Source: S2, s.6(1)–(2). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("clear, informed"; purpose limitation and data minimisation principles). The full formula itself is single-source.

16. **Withdrawal.** Consent can be withdrawn at any time, with ease comparable to how it was given (s.6(4)). Withdrawal does not affect the legality of earlier processing (s.6(5)). Processing must stop within a reasonable time (s.6(6)).
    - Source: S2, s.6(4)–(6). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S1 Rule 3(c)(i) and S3.

17. **Burden of proof on the fiduciary.** In any proceeding, the fiduciary must prove that notice was given and that consent was obtained in line with the Act and Rules. This implies keeping auditable consent and notice evidence.
    - Source: S2, s.6(10). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: single-source (statute text).

18. **Consent Managers.**
    - Consent may be given, managed, reviewed or withdrawn through a Consent Manager registered with the Board (s.6(7)–(9)).
    - Conditions for registration (First Schedule Part A): a company incorporated in India; net worth of at least ₹2 crore; sound management; independent certification of an interoperable platform.
    - Obligations (Part B): the Consent Manager cannot read the data it routes; it must keep records of consents, notices and sharing for at least 7 years; it cannot sub-contract; it acts in a fiduciary capacity.
    - Source: S1, Rule 4 and First Schedule; S2, s.6(7)–(9). Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("Consent Managers ... must be companies based in India") and #3 (Rule 4 takes effect at 12 months).

19. **Legitimate uses (s.7).** Processing without consent is allowed only for the listed legitimate uses:
    - (a) a specified purpose for which the Data Principal voluntarily provided her data to the fiduciary and has not objected;
    - (b)–(d) State functions and legal disclosure;
    - (e) court orders;
    - (f)–(h) medical emergencies, epidemics and disasters;
    - (i) employment.

    None of these is a general "legitimate interest" or marketing ground.
    - Source: S2, s.7. Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: single-source (statute text).

20. **Inference: cold outreach needs consent.** This is an inference from #19. Cold outreach to leads on a purchased or third-party list does not fit s.7(a), because the data was not voluntarily provided by the principal to the calling fiduciary for that purpose. Such outreach would therefore need s.6 consent, preceded by a s.5 notice.

    Inbound enquiries are different. The illustration to s.7(a) describes a person who messages a broker asking for help. Following up such an enquiry can fit s.7(a) until the person says she no longer wants it.
    - Source: derived from S2, s.7(a) and its illustrations.
    - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (inference).
    - Two-source status: single-source. No law-firm opinion on lead lists was read.

21. **Publicly available data.** The Act does not apply to personal data that the Data Principal herself made publicly available, or that someone legally obliged to publish it made public. This could be relevant to scraped or publicly listed contacts.
    - Source: S2, s.3(c)(ii). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high on the text; low on how it applies to lead lists.
    - Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: single-source.

### E. Purpose limitation, accuracy, retention, logs and security

22. **Accuracy.** Where personal data is used to make a decision affecting the Data Principal, or is disclosed to another fiduciary, the fiduciary must ensure it is complete, accurate and consistent.
    - Source: S2, s.8(3). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 (accuracy principle).

23. **Erasure.** The fiduciary must erase personal data when consent is withdrawn, or as soon as it is reasonable to assume the specified purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, unless a law requires retention. The purpose is deemed no longer served if the principal neither approaches the fiduciary nor exercises her rights for a prescribed period.
    - Source: S2, s.8(7)–(8) and s.8(11). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by #24 (Rule 8 operationalises it) and S3 (storage limitation).

24. **Specified retention periods and the 48-hour warning (Rule 8(1)–(2), Third Schedule).** These apply only to three classes of fiduciary:
    - e-commerce entities with at least 2 crore registered users in India;
    - online gaming intermediaries with at least 50 lakh users;
    - social media intermediaries with at least 2 crore users.

    For them, the period is three years from the principal's last approach or from commencement, whichever is latest. They must warn the principal at least 48 hours before erasure. A B2B calling SaaS and its business customers generally fall outside these classes, and the general s.8(7) test applies to them.
    - Source: S1, Rule 8(1)–(2) and Third Schedule. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high on the text; medium on the classification of a B2B calling SaaS.
    - Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: single-source for the Third Schedule detail.

25. **Minimum one-year retention of data and logs.** Every fiduciary must keep the personal data, associated traffic data and processing logs for at least one year from the date of processing. This covers its own processing and processing by its processors. The purposes are those in the Seventh Schedule, such as State security and legal functions and the assessment of SDFs. After that the data must be erased unless another law requires longer retention.

    Illustration Case 2 in the Rules says the fiduciary must ensure that its cloud processor also keeps data and logs for at least one year.
    - Source: S1, Rule 8(3) and Seventh Schedule. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S4 ("PD, associated traffic data, and other logs ... at least one year").

26. **Security safeguards (Rule 6(1)(a)–(g)).** They must include at minimum:
    - encryption, obfuscation, masking or tokenisation;
    - access control;
    - logging, monitoring and review to detect unauthorised access;
    - backups and continuity measures;
    - retention of such logs and personal data for one year, unless another law requires otherwise;
    - security provisions in processor contracts;
    - technical and organisational measures.
    - Source: S1, Rule 6. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S4 and #8 (s.8(5)).

27. **Breach notification.**
    - Each affected principal must be told without delay what happened, the likely consequences, the mitigations, the steps she can take, and a contact person.
    - The Board must receive an initial report without delay, and a detailed report within 72 hours, extendable by the Board on a written request.
    - Source: S1, Rule 7(1)–(2). Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 (breach notification to all affected individuals "without delay").

28. **Penalties (Act Schedule).**
    - Failure to keep reasonable security safeguards: up to ₹250 crore.
    - Failure to notify a breach, and breach of the children's obligations: up to ₹200 crore each.
    - Any other violation: up to ₹50 crore.
    - Source: S3; S2 Schedule, items 1–7. Pub date: 2025-11-17. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: enforcement / quantitative.
    - Two-source status: confirmed (S2 Schedule and S3).

### F. Rights, grievances, nomination, children

29. **Right of access (s.11).** The principal may obtain a summary of her data and the processing activities, and the identities of all fiduciaries and processors with whom her data was shared, with a description of the data shared.
    - Source: S2, s.11(1). Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("Right to Know How Data is Used", "Right to Access").

30. **Correction and erasure (s.12).** The principal may have her data corrected, completed, updated and erased. An erasure request must be honoured unless retention is necessary for the specified purpose or for legal compliance.
    - Source: S2, s.12. Pub date: 2023-08-11. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3.

31. **How rights are exercised (Rule 14).**
    - Fiduciaries and Consent Managers must publish how to make a rights request and what identifiers are needed.
    - They must publish the response period for grievances, which may not exceed 90 days, and take measures to meet it.
    - Principals may nominate one or more individuals to exercise their rights (Rule 14(4); s.14 limits this to death or incapacity).
    - Source: S1, Rule 14(1)–(5); S2, s.14. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("respond ... within ninety days") and S4 ("no later than ninety days").

32. **Grievance first, then the Board.** The principal must use the fiduciary's grievance mechanism before approaching the Board (s.13(3)). The fiduciary must publish the contact of a DPO or of a person who can answer questions on processing, and must mention it in every response to a rights request (Rule 9).
    - Source: S2, s.13 and s.8(9)–(10); S1, Rule 9. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed (S1 and S2 independently; S3 "Clear Contact for Queries").

33. **Children.** A "child" is anyone under 18.
    - Verifiable parental consent is required before processing any child's personal data (s.9(1)).
    - The fiduciary must check that the parent is an identifiable adult, using identity and age details it already holds reliably, or a virtual token such as DigiLocker (Rule 10).
    - Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited (s.9(3)).
    - The exemptions in the Fourth Schedule cover health, education, child care and State functions, not commercial outreach.
    - Source: S2, s.2(f) and s.9; S1, Rules 10 and 12 and Fourth Schedule. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 ("verifiable consent from a parent or guardian is required").

34. **Board inquiries.** The Board must complete an inquiry within 6 months of receiving an intimation or complaint, extendable by up to 3 months at a time. The Board works as a digital office. Appeals go to TDSAT.
    - Source: S1, Rules 19(9), 20 and 22. Pub date: 2025-11-13. Accessed: 2026-09-25.
    - Confidence: high. Class: enforcement.
    - Two-source status: confirmed by S3 (digital Board; TDSAT appeals). The 6-month limit is single-source.

## Glossary

- **Data Fiduciary:** a person who, alone or with others, determines the purpose and means of processing personal data. (S2 s.2(i); S3)
- **Data Processor:** a person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary. (S2 s.2(k))
- **Data Principal:** the individual the data relates to; for a child, this includes the parent or lawful guardian. (S2 s.2(j); S3)
- **Child:** an individual who has not completed 18 years. (S2 s.2(f))
- **Consent Manager:** a person registered with the Board who acts as a single point of contact through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform for giving, managing, reviewing and withdrawing consent. (S2 s.2(g))
- **Specified purpose:** the purpose stated in the notice given by the fiduciary. (S2 s.2(za))
- **Personal data:** any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to that data. (S2 s.2(t))
- **Processing:** a wholly or partly automated operation on digital personal data, including collection, recording, storage, use, sharing and erasure. (S2 s.2(x))
- **Personal data breach:** unauthorised processing, or accidental disclosure, loss and similar events, that compromises the confidentiality, integrity or availability of data. (S2 s.2(u))
- **Certain legitimate uses:** the non-consent grounds listed in s.7. (S2 s.2(d), s.7)
- **Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF):** a fiduciary notified under s.10 on factors such as volume and sensitivity of data and risk. It has DPO, audit and DPIA duties (Rule 13: DPIA and audit every 12 months). (S2 s.10; S1 R.13)
- **Verifiable consent:** consent as specified in Rule 10 (parent of a child) or Rule 11 (guardian of a person with disability). (S1 R.2(1)(d))
- **User account:** an online account, including an email address or mobile number, through which the principal accesses the fiduciary's services. (S1 R.2(1)(c))
- **Eighth Schedule languages:** the constitutionally scheduled Indian languages; the notice and consent request must be offered in English or any of them. (S2 s.5(3), s.6(3))

## Leads

### Contradictions (first)

- **C1: notification date and deadlines differ by one day.**
  - The Gazette notification (S1) is dated 13 Nov 2025.
  - PIB (S3) and Shardul (S4) say the Rules were notified or commenced on 14 Nov 2025, and give the later dates as 14 Nov 2026 and 14 May 2027.
  - S5 and several snippets use 13 Nov 2026 and 13 May 2027.

  Rule 1 ties commencement to the "date of publication in the Official Gazette". The e-Gazette publication date needs checking. Until then, the engine should treat 13 May 2027 as the conservative outer date.

- **C2: status and scope of the proposed acceleration.**
  - S5 says the 18→12-month cut would apply only to SDFs.
  - Some aggregator snippets (dcomply, macksofy) present it as a general deadline and advise planning for Nov 2026.
  - No notified amendment was found. Its status on 2026-09-25 is unknown and is the most important open item. Check egazette.gov.in and meity.gov.in for a DPDP (Amendment) Rules 2026.
- **C3: erase when the purpose ends, but keep for at least a year.** s.8(7) requires erasure once the purpose is served, whichever of that or withdrawal comes first. Rule 8(3) requires personal data, traffic data and logs to be kept for at least one year from processing, and then erased. S5 reports a proposal to restate this as one year from the last date of processing. How withdrawal requests interact with the one-year hold needs legal confirmation.

### New threads

1. **Act section commencement notification.** Retrieve the S.O. that maps Act sections to the three dates. S4 gives only a summary.
2. **Law-firm analysis of purchased and third-party lead lists** under s.6/s.7, and of the "publicly available" exclusion in s.3(c)(ii) (for example, LinkedIn or directory data). Check Cyril Amarchand, Khaitan, AZB, Trilegal and IAPP.
3. **Role allocation for AI SaaS.** Is the platform a processor or a joint fiduciary when it uses transcripts for model improvement? Also, whether the Board or MeitY FAQs address this. MeitY published FAQs alongside the Rules; they were not read.
4. **Whether a voice-call consent** can meet the standard of a "clear affirmative action" plus the Rule 3 itemised notice with a website link. Does an oral notice with an SMS link suffice? This bears on getting consent during the call itself.
5. **SDF notification.** Whether any class, such as AI system operators, was notified as an SDF in 2026 (S5 lists AI systems as likely candidates).
6. **Interplay with TRAI's TCCCPR** (DND, consent registry) for telemarketing consent. This belongs to another dimension, but consent artefacts may be shared.

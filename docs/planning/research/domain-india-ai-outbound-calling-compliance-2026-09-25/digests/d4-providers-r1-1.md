Accessed 2026-09-25. Budget: 16 research tool calls used, about 12 distinct sources. ## Claims

### Exotel

1. Exotel's KYC for telephony accounts depends on the kind of business. Registered companies give a Certificate of Incorporation, a PAN card and address proof (GST/MSME certificate or utility bill). Sole proprietors give a GST/MSME or shop-establishment certificate, the proprietor's PAN, and an Aadhaar card, voter ID, passport or driving licence. Partnerships/LLPs give a partnership deed or LLP certificate plus PAN. NGOs and trusts give a trust deed or society registration plus PAN and address proof.
   - source: https://docs.exotel.com/business-phone-system/kyc-verification · publisher: Exotel (docs) · last-updated 2026-07-29 · accessed 2026-09-25 · confidence: high · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source (the DLT KYC list in #3 overlaps in part but covers a different process)
2. Exotel says IP-PSTN ExoPhones can be used only in the region where the customer completed KYC ("you can only use IP-PSTN-based Exophones from the region where you have done your KYC").
   - source: same as #1 · Exotel docs · 2026-07-29 · confidence: high · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source
3. For DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology) registration, Exotel tells customers to register their own business on an operator's DLT portal as a Principal Entity. The documents are business PAN, GST or TAN certificate, a letter of authorisation, and a government ID for the authorised person. The customer then gets a Principal Entity ID (PE ID) to use for header, template and consent registration and must supply it when sending through Exotel. These pages cover SMS; I found no voice-specific wording.
   - source: https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/3000101739-company-entity-registration-on-operator-s-website ; https://developer.exotel.com/docs/sms-support/dlt-registration (snippet) · Exotel support + developer docs · date not captured · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: confirmed by two Exotel pages of different kinds (support centre and developer docs), same publisher
4. Exotel describes the 140 series as the numbering for promotional calls and recommends it for promotion-heavy use cases that draw DND complaints: "if you are a promotion heavy use case and are receiving a lot of DND complaints, we request adopting 140 number based calling."
   - source: https://exotel.com/blog/decoding-new-calling-regulations-of-140-160-calling/ · Exotel blog · published 2024-11-19 · confidence: medium (blog register, about 10 months old) · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: loosely supported by #6 (third-party snippet)
5. In November 2024 Exotel told customers that "you may continue to use SDCA (City / Normal number) for outbound calling" if they are not receiving DND complaints. It also said "160 number calling and its final directions are yet to be released by TRAI."
   - source: same as #4 · 2024-11-19 · confidence: low-medium (possibly stale: TRAI 2025 changes are reported in #6) · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source; conflicts in part with #6 (see Leads)
6. A search summary, mixing Exotel and third-party (Acefone) pages, says three things: promotional calls must come from the 140 series and are scrubbed against the NCPR (National Customer Preference Register, India's DND list) unless there is explicit, verifiable consent; service and transactional calls use the 160 series, which is not scrubbed against DND; and a 2025 TRAI amendment set the complaint threshold at 5 complaints in 10 days, with a 15-day suspension of outgoing services for a first offence and a one-year disconnection and blacklist for repeat offences.
   - source: https://www.acefone.com/blog/140-number-series/ ; https://exotel.com/blog/outbound-call-center-guide-to-streamline-your-business/ (snippet; which page said what is unclear) · Acefone / Exotel blogs · dates not captured · confidence: low (snippet, marketing register, attribution mixed) · class: regulatory (reported) · two-source: single-source; hand to D1/D2 to check against TRAI primary text
7. Exotel's support centre says caller ID masking is illegal in India. On outbound calls the customer sees the ExoPhone (the Exotel-provisioned landline) or one of its redundant backup numbers.
   - source: https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/3000018241-everything-you-need-to-know-about-outbound-calls-on-exotel · Exotel support · modified 2023-03-05 (older than 18 months, possibly stale) · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source
8. Exotel's Voicebot applet (part of AgentStream) streams call audio in both directions over a WebSocket (wss/https URL set in the applet). The audio is raw/slin 16-bit, 8 kHz, mono little-endian PCM, base64-encoded in JSON messages, with events Connected, Start, Media, DTMF, Stop and Clear. The Stream applet is one-way; only the Voicebot applet accepts media back from the bot.
   - source: https://developer.exotel.com/docs/agentstream/stream-voicebot-applet ; https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/3000108630-working-with-the-stream-and-voicebot-applet (snippet) · Exotel developer docs + support · dates not captured · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy (technical constraint) · two-source: confirmed by two Exotel pages of different kinds
9. Exotel streaming sessions can last up to 60 minutes, and a session fails if the bot server does not respond within 10 seconds. One "updated extension guide" is labelled Beta.
   - source: same as #8, plus https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/3000132302-updated-extension-guide-working-with-the-stream-and-voicebot-applet-beta- (snippet) · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source (snippet)

### Sarvam AI

10. Sarvam AI's privacy policy says it uses "Your content (including inputs, uploads, prompts, or generated outputs) to train, fine-tune, and/or improve our AI models unless you explicitly opt-out through your account settings" or by email. **Training is on by default and needs an opt-out.**
    - source: https://www.sarvam.ai/privacy-policy · Sarvam AI (legal) · updated 2026-07-29 · confidence: high that the text says this; medium on whether it covers API traffic, because the policy does not separate API from consumer products · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source; conflicts in part with #13
11. The same policy sets retention of user content (inputs/outputs) as user-configurable, defaulting to 30 days after last access. The API docs say retention can be set per workspace with an optional per-product override.
    - source: #10 URL, plus https://docs.sarvam.ai/ (snippet) · confidence: medium-high · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: confirmed by #10 and the Sarvam docs (different kind of page, same publisher)
12. Sarvam's policy says personal data may be transferred to and processed outside India: in the United States (AWS, GCP and Azure cloud infrastructure, plus analytics providers) and in the EU (certain model providers and security vendors). Payment data and Content Studio voice biometrics are stored in India. It also mentions data processing agreements with international vendors and SCCs (Standard Contractual Clauses), and names Axonwise Private Limited (doing business as Sarvam AI) as the Data Fiduciary under the DPDP Act, 2023.
    - source: #10 URL · 2026-07-29 · confidence: high · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source
13. Sarvam's custom-model-training product page says training data "is not pooled with other customers or used to train Sarvam models" and that training runs on infrastructure in India. This applies to paid custom training engagements only.
    - source: https://www.sarvam.ai/products/model-training (snippet) · marketing page · date not captured · confidence: low-medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source

### LLM in-region options

14. On Bedrock in India (ap-south-1 Mumbai, ap-south-2 Hyderabad), Claude models (Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5) are offered only through **Global** cross-Region inference (CRIS). Requests may therefore be processed in AWS commercial Regions outside India, while CloudWatch and CloudTrail logs stay in the source Region.
    - source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/access-anthropic-claude-models-in-india-on-amazon-bedrock-with-global-cross-region-inference/ · AWS ML blog · published 2026-03-09 (more than 6 months old, so possibly stale for AI claims) · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source; the Bedrock inference-profiles docs page was not fetched
15. Bedrock has India **geographic** inference profiles that route only between Mumbai and Hyderabad. OpenAI GPT-5.6 models (profiles `in.openai.gpt-5.6-terra` and `in.openai.gpt-5.6-luna`) became available with in-country inference in India in August 2026.
    - source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/introducing-openai-models-on-amazon-bedrock-for-in-country-inferencing-in-india/ ; https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/08/amazon-bedrock-openai-india-v1/ (snippets) · AWS blog + What's New · 2026-08 · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: confirmed by two AWS pages of different kinds
16. OpenAI lists India as a data-residency region for the API Platform, enabled by creating a new API Project with India selected. Requests in such Projects are handled in-region, and model requests and responses are not stored at rest on OpenAI's servers. Eligibility rules apply and the feature goes through sales.
    - source: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10503543-data-residency-for-the-openai-api ; https://openai.com/index/introducing-data-residency-in-asia/ (snippets) · OpenAI help centre + announcement · dates not captured · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: confirmed by two OpenAI pages of different kinds
17. On Anthropic's own API, the `inference_geo` parameter accepts only "us" or "global", and "us" is the only workspace geo. US-only inference on models released after 2026-02-01 costs 1.1× on all token categories. **There is no India inference-geo option.**
    - source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency (snippet) · Anthropic docs · date not captured · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: single-source (the cookbook page is the same publisher and was not fetched)

### Clerk

18. Clerk offers no regional data residency or region selection. Data is hosted on US infrastructure (Google Cloud and Cloudflare, with subprocessors in the USA). Clerk publishes a DPA covering GDPR (via DPF (Data Privacy Framework) and SCCs), CCPA, LGPD, APPI and Australia. The DPA does not mention India or the DPDP Act.
    - source: https://clerk.com/legal/dpa ; https://clerk.com/security (snippets) · Clerk legal + security pages · dates not captured · confidence: medium · class: gatekeeper-policy · two-source: confirmed by two Clerk pages of different kinds

## Leads

**Contradictions**

- **Sarvam training (#10 vs #13):** The privacy policy (2026-07-29) says content trains models unless the customer opts out. The custom-training page says customer data is not used to train Sarvam models, but only for custom engagements. It is unclear whether API STT/TTS traffic falls under the opt-out default. Next step: fetch the Sarvam API Terms of Service or DPA, check for an org-level training opt-out in the dashboard, and ask Sarvam sales in writing.
- **Exotel regular numbers and 160 series (#5 vs #6):** Exotel's November 2024 blog says SDCA numbers can still be used and 160 directions were not yet out. The later third-party material reports the 160 series as live and a stricter 2025 TRAI regime. The Exotel blog is probably stale. Needs TRAI primary text (D1/D2) and a current Exotel page.
- **Bedrock residency (#14 vs #15):** India geo profiles exist, but as of March 2026 Claude was reachable in India only through Global CRIS. "Bedrock in ap-south-1" does not by itself keep Claude inference in India. Check the current `inference-profiles-support` doc for whether an `in.anthropic.*` profile now exists.

**New threads**

1. Exotel voice-side DLT: whether the customer (principal entity), Exotel or the platform must register as Telemarketer or Principal Entity for voice, and whether Exotel scrubs NCPR automatically on 140-series calls. Try the Exotel TRAI and commercial-communications support pages and the Exotel 140-number onboarding pages.
2. Exotel call-recording storage region and retention, and Exotel's DPA and subprocessors (trust/legal pages), were not found.
3. Anthropic via Bedrock versus direct API: neither gives India in-region processing for Claude as of this run. OpenAI (direct API India residency, or Bedrock `in.` profiles) is currently the evidenced route for in-India LLM inference. Azure OpenAI Central India and Vertex asia-south1 still need checking.
4. Clerk is US-only. Storing Indian users' identity data with Clerk means a cross-border transfer; D1 should confirm the DPDP cross-border rules (negative-list approach) for the auth-data decision.
5. The 8 kHz narrowband audio from the Exotel stream (#8) limits STT choice; check that Sarvam STT accepts 8 kHz PCM.

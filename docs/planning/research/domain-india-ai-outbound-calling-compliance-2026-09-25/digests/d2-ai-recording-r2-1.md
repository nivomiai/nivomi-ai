## Claims

1. On 21 June 2024 DoT published a Gazette notification bringing sections 1, 2, 10–30, 42–44, 46, 47, 50–58, 61 and 62 of the Telecommunications Act, 2023 into force from 26 June 2024. Sections 20 (interception/suspension) and 28 (user protection) both fall inside the 10–30 range.
   - Sources: https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Update-Staggered-Enforcement-of-Telecommunications-Act-2023.pdf (AZB & Partners, 2024-06-25); https://trilegal.com/knowledge_repository/trilegal-update-government-notifies-sections-of-the-telecommunications-act-2023/ (Trilegal, 2024-07-26; the landing page confirms the notification but its section list came only through the search snippet); CyberPeace and Lexology search snippets repeat the same list.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: YES (AZB + Trilegal/CyberPeace).
2. A second notification dated 4 July 2024 brought sections 6–8, 48 and 59(b) into force from 5 July 2024. The spectrum-auction provisions, adjudication provisions and TRAI Act amendments stayed un-notified at that point.
   - Sources: CyberPeace blog https://cyberpeace.org/resources/blogs/the-government-enforces-key-sections-of-the-telecommunication-act-2023 (search snippet only, not fetched); Trilegal snippet.
   - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE in substance, because the snippets were not verified by fetching. No later (2025–2026) commencement notification was found; see Looked for.
3. Section 28 is in force as an enabling power. It lets the Central Government make rules requiring prior user consent to receive "specified messages" (marketing, promotional, solicitation or advertising messages) and requiring Do-Not-Disturb registers. As of June 2024 no rules had been made under it. The existing regime is TRAI's Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018 (TCCCPR), made under the TRAI Act.
   - Source: AZB PDF above, §8.1–8.2 (text extracted).
   - Confidence: high for the content of the power; low for "still no rules as of 2026-09" (not searched this round).
   - Class: regulatory. Two-source: STILL SINGLE-SOURCE (AZB only).
4. Section 20 of the Act (in force) lets the Central or State Government, or authorised officers, direct that messages be intercepted, restricted or disclosed on public-safety and related grounds, subject to prescribed safeguards. It is a state power over messages carried on networks. Nothing retrieved describes it as covering a participant's own recording of a call.
   - Source: AZB PDF §6.
   - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE.
5. Participant ("one-party") recording is widely described as effectively lawful in India. No statute expressly requires all-party consent. Criminal and constitutional exposure (Telegraph Act s.25; Article 21 privacy) is said to attach to non-participant interception. Only the State may intercept, under Telegraph Act s.5(2) or IT Act s.69.
   - Sources: https://recordinglaw.com/india-recording-laws/ (Recording Law, 2026, aggregator); https://www.sansalegal.com/post/is-it-legal-to-record-a-phone-call-in-india-consent-and-privacy-explained (Sansa Legal, n.d.). Both seen as search snippets only.
   - Confidence: low. Class: regulatory/case-law. Two-source: nominally two publishers, but both are low-tier explainer or marketing sites, so treat as UNVERIFIED.
   - Round-1 hypothesis "participant recording is not 'interception'": STILL SINGLE-SOURCE (weak). No primary text or top-tier firm analysis was retrieved.
6. MeitY notified the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 by Gazette notification G.S.R. 120(E), published 10 February 2026 (gazette ID CG-DL-E-10022026-269993). The rules took effect on 20 February 2026.
   - Sources: https://gazettetracker.com/g/CG-DL-E-10022026-269993 (Gazette Tracker index of the eGazette; the ID encodes 10-02-2026); https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=adb2714c-1188-43e3-b62b-eb6649727f6e (Lexology/Kan and Krishme, 2026). The Esya Centre analysis is dated 2026-02-11, one day after the notification, which is consistent.
   - Confidence: high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: YES.
   - Round-1 date conflict (10 vs 16 Feb): RESOLVED to 10 Feb 2026. The 16 Feb date appears in Khaitan's note and is probably that note's own date or a republication, not the notification date.
7. "Synthetically generated information" (SGI) means audio, visual or audio-visual information created or altered artificially or algorithmically using a computer resource, so that it appears real, authentic or true and is indistinguishable from a natural person or real-world event.
   - Exclusions: text-only content, routine or good-faith editing, preparation of documents, and accessibility, clarity, quality, translation or searchability uses.
   - Source: https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leadership/MeitY-notifies-the-IT-Amendment-Rules-2026 (Khaitan & Co, 2026-02-16).
   - Confidence: medium-high. Class: definition. Two-source: SINGLE-SOURCE for the exact wording. Lexology/Mondaq snippets describe SGI generally, but one says it includes "AI-generated text", which contradicts the text-only exclusion; see Leads.
8. Audio SGI must carry a prominently prefixed audio disclosure. This duty sits on intermediaries that offer a computer resource enabling creation, modification or dissemination of SGI. Significant social media intermediaries have extra duties: user declarations, verification and labels (new Rule 4(1A)).
   - Sources: Khaitan (above); Digital Policy Alert / Gazette Tracker search snippet (Rule 3(3) technical measures, labelling and metadata; Rule 4(1A)).
   - Confidence: medium-high. Class: regulatory. Two-source: YES for labelling and SSMI duties; SINGLE-SOURCE for the "prefixed audio disclosure" wording.
9. Neither retrieved analysis of the 2026 amendment mentions telecom services, voice calls or telephony.
   - Sources: Khaitan (fetched and checked explicitly); Lexology and Mondaq snippets.
   - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory. Two-source: partial.
   - Round-1 hypothesis "binds intermediaries, not phone calls": CONFIRMED in part. Nothing extends it to calls. However, a SaaS platform that generates synthetic voice could arguably be an "intermediary offering a computer resource enabling creation of SGI"; this is untested (see Leads).
10. In September 2026 the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) published draft "Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Pesky, Promotional and Unsolicited Commercial Communications, 2026" under s.18(2) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, with enforcement through ss.20–21. Their scope covers voice calls and SMS on telecom networks, OTT calling and messaging apps, RCS and social media.
    - Sources: https://www.medianama.com/2026/09/223-ccpa-draft-guidelines-spam-ai-promotions/ (MediaNama, 2026-09-24); https://www.adgully.com/post/20560/ccpa-drafts-strict-rules-to-curb-pesky-calls-ott-ads-and-ai-marketing (Adgully, 2026-09).
    - Confidence: medium-high. Class: regulatory (draft, not binding). Two-source: YES (two independent media outlets; primary CCPA/consumeraffairs text not retrieved).
11. The CCPA 2026 draft would require businesses to disclose at the outset that a promotional communication uses an artificial, synthetic or AI-generated voice, audibly on voice calls. MediaNama's example: a bank's telemarketer cannot promote loans with a synthetic voice without disclosing its artificial origin.
    - Sources: MediaNama (fetched, quoted); Adgully and retailintel snippets ("restrictions on AI-generated promotional calls", "AI disclosure").
    - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (draft). Two-source: YES for "AI disclosure proposed"; SINGLE-SOURCE (MediaNama) for "audible, at the outset".
    - Round-1 hypothesis "no Indian rule requires telling the recipient they are speaking to an AI": still true for BINDING rules, but OVERTURNED as a forward-looking position. A draft requiring exactly this is out for consultation.
12. The draft would treat any commercial communication sent without explicit, verifiable (digital) consent, or against a registered opt-out, as an unsolicited commercial communication. That would count as an unfair trade practice or misleading advertisement.
    - Its terms: consent must be per brand or beneficiary and per product; opt-out must be free and simple; after opt-out a cooling-off period applies (45, 60 or 90 days) during which fresh consent may not be sought.
    - It binds senders, those who engage senders, intended beneficiaries and brands named in the communications.
    - Exclusions: transactional messages (OTPs, banking alerts, order and delivery updates), government communications, existing contractual communications and employer-employee communications.
    - Sources: MediaNama (fetched); Adgully (snippet).
    - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (draft). Two-source: YES for consent and UCC classification and the exclusions; SINGLE-SOURCE for the cooling-off figures and the list of liable parties.
13. An earlier CCPA draft, "Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Unsolicited and Unwarranted Business Communication, 2024", was put out for public feedback in 2024.
    - Sources: https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-invites-public-feedback-on-draft-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-unsolicited-and-unwarranted-business-communication-2024 (All India Radio News, government broadcaster); https://www.civis.vote/consultations/798 (Civis). Both seen as search snippets.
    - Confidence: medium. Class: regulatory (draft). Two-source: YES (snippets).
    - Whether the 2026 draft supersedes it was not stated in MediaNama.

## Glossary

- **Specified message (Telecom Act s.28)**: a marketing, promotional, solicitation or advertising message. Rules may require prior consent and a DND register.
- **TCCCPR 2018**: TRAI's Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, the operative Indian UCC/DND regime under the TRAI Act.
- **SGI**: synthetically generated information under the IT Rules 2026 amendment (see claim 7).
- **SSMI**: significant social media intermediary (above a user-count threshold), with extra IT Rules duties.
- **UCC**: unsolicited commercial communication.
- **CCPA (India)**: Central Consumer Protection Authority under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Not the California law.
- **Participant / one-party recording**: recording by a party to the call, as opposed to interception by a third party.

Decision this research serves: Contents of the India rule set for the ComplianceEngine (ADR-014) and the compliance constraints the PRD must carry

Research, not legal advice. Every conclusion below should be confirmed by Indian telecom and data-protection counsel before launch. Confidence is marked per claim: high = verified by two independent publishers (or primary text + independent analysis) and fresh; medium = one credible source; low = weak, stale or disputed source. Compound labels (for example medium-high) sit between two levels; qualifiers such as "as text", "as draft text" or "TRAI text only" say what the rating covers; "absence" means nothing was found after a search; and inferences are labelled as such. Abbreviations are defined in the Glossary.

Executive summary

What the evidence says to do: build the India rule set around the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India's (TRAI) commercial-communication regulations (TCCCPR 2018 as amended in February 2025 and, once gazetted, September 2026), design the data layer now for the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act and Rules that bind from about 13 May 2027, and ship the AI-voice disclosure and recording notice by default even though no binding rule requires them yet.

The findings that drive this:

  1. Nivomi's MVP calls are promotional commercial calls, and they are heavily regulated. A sales or lead-qualification call — including a callback to a prospect who inquired but has not bought — is a Promotional Voice Call; any promotional content makes the whole call promotional [1][6]. Promotional calls may only originate from 140xx numbers of a sender registered on the operators' distributed-ledger (DLT) platform, and reach a subscriber only if they have not blocked promotions or have given registered explicit consent [1][2][5]. Ordinary 10-digit numbers are not allowed for promotional calls high.
  2. An AI voice agent makes automated application-to-person (A2P) or robo calls. TRAI's final Third Amendment (adopted 18 Sep 2026, not yet in the Gazette) defines A2P calls to include "pre-recorded/artificial voice" and treats every undeclared A2P call as unsolicited commercial communication (UCC) [2][3][16]; the "undeclared = UCC" consequence rests on TRAI's text alone. A written advance declaration of auto-dialer/robo-call use has been required since early 2025 (notified 12 Feb 2025) [1][6].
  3. Enforcement is fast and binary. Five complaints from different people within 10 days — or three when an operator's AI/ML system has also flagged one of the sender's numbers — trigger suspension. Repeated AI flags alone start know-your-customer (KYC) re-verification. A first violation bars all of the sender's telecom resources for 15 days; a repeat means one-year disconnection and blacklisting across all operators [1][2][7]. Over 1,150 senders had been blacklisted by March 2025 [10][11]. A tenant's campaign can therefore take down that tenant's entire telephony.
  4. Consent has two independent legal regimes. Telecom consent (TRAI's registered consent, 7-day inquiry window, 90-day re-consent wait) and DPDP consent (notice + proof of consent for the purpose) are separate. TRAI's 2026 memorandum says revoking telecom consent does not revoke DPDP consent [2] (the reverse direction is not addressed). DPDP has no "legitimate interest" ground: cold calling purchased lists needs DPDP consent, while following up a person's own inquiry can arguably rely on s.7(a), which covers data the person provided voluntarily [29][36] medium, inference.
  5. No binding AI-disclosure duty exists today, but a draft is coming. A Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) draft (September 2026, working-draft status) would require an audible "this is AI" disclosure at the start of the call [18][19][20]. No authoritative source settles call-recording legality; recording is personal-data processing under DPDP [29].
  6. Several providers keep data outside India. HubSpot has no India hosting [61]; Clerk is US-only [63]; Claude has no India-only inference on Bedrock [54][56]; Sarvam's own policies contradict each other on training on customer audio [52][53]. DPDP currently permits transfers (no restricted-country list) [28][32], but sector rules may not permit them for banking, financial services and insurance (BFSI) tenants [42].

Biggest caveat: several operational facts cannot be settled from public sources — whether Nivomi must register as a telemarketer (aggregator), what Exotel does for voice DLT and 140xx/1601xx numbers, the Third Amendment's Gazette date, and whether the 7-day inquiry window overrides DND. These need written answers from Exotel and Indian counsel before the rule set is final (see Open questions).

What to do with it: the findings translate into the ADR-014 rule table (R1), PRD constraints (R2), architecture constraints (R3) and pre-build actions (R4); see Recommendations. The two conclusions that most shape the product are that consent needs two separate ledgers, and that fast follow-up of inbound leads is the lawful MVP journey.

1. Telecom commercial-communication rules (TRAI)

Nivomi's sales calls are promotional, automated commercial calls: they need a registered sender, 140xx numbers, a declared automated-calling setup and a consent or preference basis, and complaints can shut a sender down within days.

1.1 Instruments and their status

InstrumentStatus on 2026-09-25Confidence

Third Amendment commencement runs from Gazette publication: +30 days for most provisions (including the new 7-day inquiry "Relationship" and the legacy-consent rule), +60 days for the A2P declaration, VNO interface, header/template misuse regime and the A2P termination charge, and +90 days for the Annexure items (consumer appeal, AI-flag corroboration) [2][5]. MediaNama places the inquiry window at +60 days; the regulation's own commencement clause says +30 [2][5] — the regulation governs.

1.2 Call categories and number series

  • Promotional Voice Call — any voice commercial communication with promotional content; if promotional content is mixed into any commercial call, the call is promotional [1][6] high.
  • Prospects are promotional recipients. TRAI's explanatory memorandum says communications to people "who have shown interest in a product or service but have not yet made a purchase" belong under promotional [1] high as TRAI's stated position; single primary source.
  • Service Voice Call — (i) to the sender's existing customer about a product or service already procured, no explicit consent needed; (ii) to facilitate an ongoing purchase, made with explicit consent that is valid for 7 days and that the customer can renew 7 days at a time (renewal added in 2026) [1][2][7] (7-day validity high; renewal medium).
  • Transactional Voice Call — in response to a customer-initiated transaction within 30 minutes [1] medium, single primary.
  • Number series: promotional calls only from 140xx; service/transactional from 1600xx for BFSI and government, 1601xx for other sectors [2][5] (140xx/1600xx high; 1601xx split medium, TRAI text only). BFSI entities regulated by RBI, SEBI and PFRDA had phased 1600 mandates between 1 Jan and 15 Mar 2026 [8][9] (Direction high; dates medium-high, SEBI/PFRDA dates from [9] only); insurers reportedly by 15 Feb 2026 low — press snippets only, not in the appendix. Commercial calls may use only registered headers or special series (reg 3(1)) [1][13], so no ordinary 10-digit numbers for promotional calls high; see Contrary evidence for the argument about non-promotional calls.
  • Caller-ID/spam apps may not blanket-block or spam-tag 140xx, 1600xx or 1601xx numbers (reg 34A) [2][5] high.

1.3 Registration, DLT and the telemarketer chain

  • An unregistered sender must not make any commercial communication; its resources may be suspended or disconnected (reg 3(2)) [1][12] high.
  • Registration requires physical verification of the entity, biometric authentication of the authorised person, and a linked mobile number [1] medium-high. Senders self-certify their registration, headers, content templates and consent templates every year or face automatic suspension [1] medium.
  • Chain limit: at most one Telemarketer with Aggregator Function and one Telemarketer with Delivery Function between sender and operator (reg 22(1)(e)) [1] medium, single primary. An aggregator pools traffic from multiple enterprises without direct operator connectivity; a delivery telemarketer has that connectivity [12] medium, 2023 operator FAQ.
  • The principal entity (the business) must file its own entity, header, content-template and consent-template registrations; a telemarketer cannot file them for it [12] medium, 2023.
  • Inference, unconfirmed: a multi-tenant platform that pools many businesses' traffic and hands it to a CPaaS provider (Exotel) matches the aggregator role, so Nivomi may need registered telemarketer (aggregator) status. No regulator or operator text addresses SaaS voice platforms low-medium.

1.4 Automated and AI calls

  • Since early 2025 (Second Amendment notified 12 Feb 2025), every sender must notify its originating operator in writing and in advance of auto-dialer or robo-call use and the calls' objective (reg 4) [1][6] high.
  • Robo Call (TCCCPR 2(bc)): a call "using an artificial or prerecorded voice to interactively deliver a voice message without the involvement of human being on calling side" [4] medium; read from TRAI's consolidated draft text.
  • A2P call (Third Amendment 2(ea)): "initiated by an application, software system, or automated platform without direct human dialling … including using autodialling, robo-calls and/or pre-recorded/artificial voice technologies" [2][16] high. The sender must declare A2P use and the calling-number (CLI) ranges to its operator, which records them on DLT [2][5][16] (declaration duty high; DLT recording TRAI text only). Undeclared A2P calls are treated as unsolicited commercial communication [2] medium — TRAI text only. TRAI's memorandum applies the declaration duty to all A2P calls, including service calls [2] medium.
  • Inference: a fully automated conversational AI agent falls within both definitions ("artificial voice", "interactively … without … human being on calling side") medium. No TRAI guidance names LLM or conversational agents. See Contrary evidence.
  • Operators may charge up to ₹0.05/min between themselves for A2P calls, except on 140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx [2][3][5][16] (charge high; series exemption TRAI text only [2][3]). This is another reason to stay on the designated series.
  • Abandoned and silent calls are defined, and operators must act against auto-dialer senders exceeding "prescribed limits"; no numeric limit was found [4] medium.

1.5 Consent, preferences and calling windows

  • Preference register (NCPR / DND), set through the 1909 short code. Subscribers can choose FULLY BLOCK, which stops promotional and explicit-consent service communications (not inferred-consent, transactional or government); BLOCK PROMO, which stops promotional only; or blocks on any of 8 content categories (1 banking/insurance/ financial, 2 real estate, 3 education, 4 health, 5 consumer goods and automobiles, 6 communication/broadcasting/entertainment/IT, 7 tourism and leisure, 8 food and beverages) [1] medium, single primary.
  • Registered explicit consent (Digital Consent Acquisition via the operators; consent requests from short code 127xxx naming brand and scope) lets promotional calls reach subscribers despite their preferences [1] high as TRAI text. From the Third Amendment, consent obtained outside that framework counts only if verifiable and registered in the Consent Register [2][5][16] high.
  • Inquiry window (Third Amendment 2(bb)(ii)): a written or digital inquiry creates a "Relationship" for 7 days; it must be kept "in a verifiable form" and need not be registered. A product application extends this to 3 months [2][16][5] (7 days high; 3 months medium). The text frames this as inferred consent but does not say which category or series applies or whether it overrides a DND block medium.
  • 90-day wait: after an opt-out or revocation the sender may not seek consent again for 90 days unless the customer opts back in [1][7] high.
  • Consents registered against a number are revoked when the number is surrendered [1] medium.
  • Telecom consent is not DPDP consent: the Third Amendment's definition of consent covers the channel only, and the Third Amendment states that nothing in TCCCPR exempts a sender from the DPDP Act [2] medium.
  • Calling windows: no fixed statutory window was found. Customer time bands 00–06, 06–08, 08–10 and 21–24 are closed to promotional calls by default (default OFF) for every subscriber, so promotional calls effectively run 10:00–21:00 unless the subscriber opts in; service and transactional calls cannot be blocked by time band [4] medium — from TRAI's consolidated draft text. Exotel's FAQ states 09:00–21:00 but is framed on the 2018 text [45] low. A vendor source reports a fixed 08:00–19:00 window for RBI-regulated debt collection [17] low. See Contrary evidence.

1.6 Complaints, detection and sanctions

  • Recipients complain within 7 days of the call [1][6] high.
  • Suspension triggers: at least 5 complaints from unique recipients in 10 days, or at least 3 only when one of the sender's numbers is also flagged by the operator's AI/ML spam detection [2][5] high.
  • Operator AI/ML flagging (reg 21A): suspected-UCC numbers are shared within 2 hours; five or more flagged numbers in 10 days escalates to KYC re-verification, then physical verification, and a 15-day bar only on mismatch or misuse. Designated series are never flagged [2][5] (flagging and 2-hour sharing high; escalation detail medium, TRAI text only). Detection signals in TRAI's 2025 text include calls to many distinct numbers, short average call duration and a low incoming-to-outgoing ratio [1] medium.
  • Sanctions: a first violation leads to outgoing services of all the sender's resources (SIMs, PRI/SIP trunks) being barred for 15 days by all operators; a subsequent violation leads to one-year disconnection, blacklisting and device blocking [1][2][7] high. Restoration costs ₹5,000 per resource, capped at ₹5 lakh [2] medium. A telemarketer that misuses headers or templates is disconnected and blacklisted for a year [2][16] high.
  • Scale: 1,150 entities blacklisted and over 18.8 lakh telecom resources disconnected as of 19 Mar 2025 [10][11] medium-high; both outlets report the same PIB release.

2. AI disclosure and call recording

2.1 AI disclosure

  • No binding AI-disclosure duty found for phone calls. TRAI's 2025 and 2026 texts mention AI only as an operator spam-detection tool and in the A2P definition [1][2] high, absence finding; the red-team search found none either.
  • IT Rules amendment 2026 (G.S.R. 120(E), notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026) requires labelling of synthetically generated audio, including a prefixed audio disclosure, but binds intermediaries; the analysis reviewed for this report never mentions telephony [21][22] high on dates; medium on scope. Whether an AI-voice SaaS counts as an intermediary enabling synthetic content is open.
  • CCPA draft "Pesky, Promotional and Unsolicited Commercial Communications Guidelines, 2026" (Consumer Protection Act s.18(2)) [18][19][20]:
    • The text retrieved is a committee working draft (open options, "(need to revise)" notes, no date or comment deadline) hosted by MediaNama; no DoCA/PIB notice was found [18] high on content; official status unconfirmed.
    • It would make it a violation to use an AI-generated voice "without clearly and audibly/visibly disclosing to the recipient, at the outset of the communication, that it is AI-generated"; the sender and the beneficiary brand would be liable [18][19][20] high as draft text.
    • It would require explicit consent per brand and product (for telecom voice, only via the operators' Digital Consent Acquisition), a free opt-out with confirmation, a cooling-off period of 45, 60 or 90 days (options the draft leaves open), identification of the caller and purpose, no persistent follow-ups after a single inquiry, complaint acknowledgement within 24 hours and resolution within 7 days, and records of consent, opt-outs and sender identity for 180 or 365 days (also left open) [18][19] high as draft text.
    • Scope caveat: its scope clause targets calls from normal 10-digit numbers not registered under TCCCPR and app/platform channels, and it excludes communication under subsisting explicit consent [18] medium. Whether it would bind a registered 140xx sender with consent is unclear.
    • It takes effect on publication with no transition period, and yields to TCCCPR/DPDP on conflict [18]. An earlier June 2024 draft exists [27]; no evidence was found that it was finalised low-medium.

2.2 Call recording

  • No top-tier legal analysis was found on whether a business may record its own calls without consent. Blog claims that one-party recording is illegal were rejected as low quality.
  • Supreme Court: Vibhor Garg v. Neha (14 Jul 2025) held secretly recorded spousal calls admissible as evidence in matrimonial proceedings, without ruling on lawfulness [23][24] high on holding; its reach to business calls is untested.
  • Telecommunications Act: the s.20 interception power is a State power over network messages [25] medium.
  • DPDP: "processing" includes recording, so recordings and transcripts carry DPDP notice and consent duties once those commence [29] high on definition.
  • CCPA draft: imposes no recording duty [18].

3. Data protection: DPDP Act 2023, DPDP Rules 2025, CERT-In

DPDP's business obligations start about 13 May 2027 and CERT-In's incident and log rules apply now; the product should be built to both from the start.

  • No amendment or date change found. A January 2026 MeitY proposal to cut the 18-month compliance period to 12 months targeted Significant Data Fiduciaries and remained a consultation [32] medium; a December 2025 corrigendum made minor corrections only [41] medium. The Supreme Court issued notice in challenges and refused an interim stay of the Act's RTI amendment [40] medium-high; no order pauses the DPDP dates. The Board had no chair or members as of 1 Aug 2026 and no enforcement actions [33] medium. Use 13 May 2027 as the conservative date (see Contrary evidence).

3.2 Roles

  • A fiduciary determines purpose and means, alone or with others; a processor processes on its behalf [29]. The fiduciary stays responsible for its processors and must use a valid contract with security clauses [28][29] high.
  • Inference: each business customer is the fiduciary for its lead data and Nivomi is its processor — only while Nivomi uses that data solely for the customer's purposes. Reusing recordings or transcripts for Nivomi's own purposes (model improvement, cross-tenant analytics) makes Nivomi a fiduciary for that use; AZB argues significant vendor autonomy over the means can make a vendor a fiduciary or joint fiduciary anyway [38] medium; see Contrary evidence.

3.3 Obligations that shape the product (from about May 2027)

  • Notice (s.5, Rule 3): stand-alone, plain language, itemised personal data and specific purposes, with a link and means to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board; available in English or any Eighth Schedule language [28][29][30] high. Nivomi's launch languages are believed to be Eighth Schedule languages (not verified this run).
  • Consent (s.6): free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, clear affirmative action; withdrawal as easy as giving; the fiduciary must prove notice and consent (s.6(10)) [29][37] high. No guidance was found on whether verbal or IVR consent on a call suffices [37] (absence).
  • Legitimate uses (s.7): none is a general marketing ground. s.7(a) covers data the person voluntarily provided for a specified purpose — arguably a follow-up to their own inquiry [29] medium, inference. Calling purchased or third-party lists needs consent — an inference from s.7 [29], consistent with [36], which holds that consent to process a number is not consent to marketing calls and that outsourcing does not shift responsibility medium; no source states the purchased-list conclusion directly.
  • Publicly available data is excluded only if the person made it public themselves or it was published under a legal duty [29][35] high; whether self-published professional profiles qualify is contested [39] medium.
  • Retention: erase when the purpose is served unless law requires retention (s.8(7)) [29]; but every fiduciary must keep personal data, traffic data and processing logs for at least one year from processing, including at its processors (Rule 8(3)) [28][31] high on text; medium that it applies to all fiduciaries.
  • Security (Rule 6): encryption/masking, access control, access logging and monitoring, backups, one-year log retention, security clauses in processor contracts [28][31] high.
  • Breach (Rule 7): tell affected people without delay; tell the Board without delay and send a detailed report within 72 hours; no materiality threshold in the text [28][30] high; threshold point is inference.
  • Rights (ss.11–14, Rule 14): access, correction, erasure, grievance first to the fiduciary; fiduciaries must publish a response period of at most 90 days [28][30][31] high.
  • Children: anyone under 18 needs verifiable parental consent; no tracking or targeted advertising [29][30] high.
  • Penalties: up to ₹250 crore (security), ₹200 crore (breach notification, children), ₹50 crore (other) [30][33] high.

3.4 Cross-border transfer

  • Rule 15 permits transfers outside India subject only to government orders about access by foreign states; no restricted-country list or order has been notified [28][32][41] high on text; medium on "none notified". See Contrary evidence.
  • For Significant Data Fiduciaries, Rule 13(4) lets the government require specified data and its traffic data to stay in India; no SDF has been designated [28][32] medium.
  • Sector rules still apply (s.16(2)): SEBI's 2023 cloud framework may require regulated entities' data to stay in India [42] medium. IRDAI record-keeping rules may do the same for insurers, and SEBI's later position is reportedly in flux low — snippet sources not in the appendix.

3.5 CERT-In Directions (in force now)

Report listed cyber incidents, including data breaches and leaks, to CERT-In within 6 hours; keep ICT system logs for a rolling 180 days within India; sync clocks to NIC/NPL NTP; name a point of contact [34] high. Whether a SaaS company is a "cloud service provider" subject to the 5-year subscriber-record duty is unsettled [34] low.

4. Providers: who does what, and where data lives

Several providers keep or process data outside India, and Exotel's and Sarvam's public documents leave compliance responsibilities and data terms open.

4.1 Exotel (telephony)

  • KYC by business type; IP-PSTN numbers usable only in the KYC region [43] medium.
  • Voice DLT and numbering are not settled by Exotel's public pages. An undated FAQ says DLT is "only required for SMS" [44], which conflicts with TRAI's registration rule for all commercial communication [1] — the regulator governs. Exotel's TCCCPR FAQ puts registration, consent records, opt-outs and "enable DND filtering" on the customer [45]. No Exotel page explains 140xx or 1601xx provisioning, its telemarketer role, or A2P declarations (absence). Its 2024 blog saying city numbers remain usable predates the 2025 amendment [51] (stale).
  • DND filtering exists (real-time NDNC checks) but may need enabling [45][46] medium-low — do not assume it is on.
  • Data: stored only in the region chosen at signup (Mumbai or Singapore); recordings kept 90 days by default, call records 1 year; deletion via support, purged in about 30 days; DPA on request for enterprise customers; no public subprocessor list [47] medium, undated page.
  • Streaming (Voicebot applet): two-way WebSocket, 16-bit mono PCM at 8 kHz by default (16 and 24 kHz supported); server clear event flushes queued audio (barge-in); sessions up to 60 minutes [48][49][50] medium-high. The applets may not be enabled by default and are switched on through Exotel [49].
  • Human handoff is documented only after the bot closes the stream: the flow moves on to a Passthru applet, then Connect or SIP; transfer during a live stream is not documented, ringing audio leaks into the stream (known issue), and outbound use is not documented [49][50] medium; Beta guide.

4.2 Sarvam (STT/TTS)

  • The Privacy Policy (29 Jul 2026) says customer content trains Sarvam's models unless the customer opts out, default retention is 30 days, and data may go to the US or EU [52] high on text. The Terms (same date) say training is "(where required) subject to your consent", Sarvam acts as processor under a DPA, data is processed and stored in India and may be transferred abroad [53] medium. The two documents conflict; the DPA is not public (/dpa returns 404).
  • The Terms prohibit TCCCPR-violating calls, recording without required notice/consent, and voice cloning without consent; liability is capped at 12 months' fees [53] high on text.

6. Cross-dimension insights

  1. The ComplianceEngine needs two consent ledgers, not one. Telecom consent (registered on the operator's Consent Register, channel-level, 90-day re-consent block) and DPDP consent (notice + purpose + proof, revocable independently) have different sources, lifetimes and revocation paths [2][29]. A single "consented" flag would be wrong under both.
  2. Fast follow-up of inbound leads is the lawful sweet spot for the MVP. TRAI's 7-day inquiry window [2] and DPDP s.7(a) for voluntarily provided data [29] both favour calling people who just asked to be contacted. Purchased lists need registered telecom consent and DPDP consent, which is costly to obtain. This fits the target persona (SMB sales teams following up leads) and argues for "speed-to-lead within 7 days, with evidence stored" as the core MVP journey.
  3. Campaign pacing is a compliance control, not just UX. Operators' AI/ML spam flags key on call volume, short calls and low inbound ratios [1][2], abandoned/silent calls are regulated [4], and 3–5 complaints in 10 days suspend the sender [2]. An AI agent that is slow to speak once the call is answered, hangs up fast or dials aggressively can get a tenant's entire telephony barred for 15 days. The engine needs per-tenant rate limits, answer-to-speech latency targets and a complaint-rate circuit breaker.
  4. Liability concentrates on the tenant; exposure concentrates on Nivomi. Sanctions fall on the registered sender (the tenant) [1], and the CCPA draft adds liability for engagers and brands [18], but a platform-level aggregator registration [1][12] would expose Nivomi's shared resources to every tenant's behaviour. Tenant isolation must extend to telecom identities: separate sender registrations, CLI ranges and declarations per tenant.
  5. Data residency is a per-tenant policy, not a platform constant. DPDP allows transfers today [28], but HubSpot, Clerk and Claude process outside India [54][61][63], while BFSI tenants — the segment TRAI regulates most tightly [8][9] — may be bound by sector localisation [42]. The LLMRouter and provider selection should honour a tenant-level "India-only" policy, which today points to OpenAI via Bedrock's in. profile or Azure regional deployments [55][58].
  6. Retention requirements converge on "at least a year, then delete". Together, DPDP Rule 8(3) (at least 1 year of data and logs) [28], CERT-In (180 days of ICT logs in India) [34], the CCPA draft (180 or 365 days of consent and opt-out records) [18] and Exotel's defaults (recordings 90 days, call records 1 year) [47] call for one retention matrix per data class.

7. Contrary evidence

The red-team pass (fresh-context skeptics, no supporting evidence given) tested the main conclusions.

  • "All AI follow-up calls are promotional" — weakened. A call with no promotional content that only facilitates an ongoing purchase, made with 7-day explicit consent, is a Service Voice Call and outside UCC [1]. MyOperator argued to TRAI that treating follow-ups to customer inquiries as UCC creates a "regulatory paradox" [14]. Weighed: the mixed-content rule makes any real sales pitch promotional [1][6], and TRAI kept the inquiry basis only as a 7-day inferred-consent window [2]. The conclusion stands for sales and qualification calls; appointment confirmation for an ongoing purchase can be a service call.
  • "10-digit numbers are barred for all commercial calls" — weakened for non-promotional calls. TRAI's 2025 memorandum says no special series had earlier been mandated for service calls, and 1600 is mandated only for BFSI and government [9][15]. Weighed: reg 3(1) requires registered headers or special series for commercial communication [1], and the 2026 memorandum names 1601xx for other sectors [2]. Treat 10-digit numbers as not permitted; confirm with counsel.
  • "AI agents are A2P/robo calls" — survives, with an edge case. The definitions hinge on "without direct human dialling" and no human "on calling side participating in the dialogue" [2][4]; the sender of a human-dialled or human-supervised call could argue that it falls outside. MyOperator asked TRAI to exclude consented service-type AI calls from bulk-A2P treatment [14]; the outcome is unknown. Weighed: fully automated outbound campaigns are squarely inside.
  • "No fixed calling window" — true for TRAI only. A vendor source reports RBI's fixed 08:00–19:00 window for debt collection [17] low. Weighed: sector overlays are needed.
  • "No AI-disclosure duty" — survives, with a forward risk. Weighed: nothing binding was found, but sector identity-disclosure duties and consumer-protection misrepresentation rules could still reach an AI that implies it is human [17] low.
  • "Nivomi is a processor" — weakened. Actual control, not the contract label, decides the role [38]. Weighed: secondary use of call data makes Nivomi a fiduciary for that use.
  • "Cross-border transfer is permitted" — weakened. Weighed: sector localisation (SEBI [42]; IRDAI, low confidence) survives DPDP, and Rule 13(4)/Rule 15 orders can add restrictions [28][32].
  • "DPDP binds from May 2027" — survives. No acceleration was notified [32][41]. Weighed: MeitY has shown intent to compress timelines, so treat May 2027 as a planning date, not a guarantee.

R2 — PRD constraints.

  • Make the MVP journey inbound-lead follow-up: leads carry a consent basis (inquiry, application, registered consent, existing customer) with evidence; imports without a basis are not callable. (Insight 2; [2][29][36], medium.)

  • Human handoff designed around Exotel's end-of-stream Passthru → Connect pattern until Exotel confirms mid-stream transfer ([50], medium).

R3 — Architecture constraints.

  • Two consent ledgers (telecom, DPDP) with independent revocation; append-only evidence store (Insight 1).
  • Per-tenant telecom identity (sender registration, CLI ranges, declarations) — tenant isolation extends to numbers (Insight 4).
  • Tenant residency policy consumed by LLMRouter and provider selection; "India-only" routes to in-country options [55][58] (Insight 5).
  • Retention matrix per data class (≥1 year data and logs, CERT-In ICT logs 180 days in India, recordings per tenant policy) [28][34]; NTP sync to NIC/NPL [34] (Insight 6).
  • No training or cross-tenant reuse of call data by Nivomi or its processors by default [38][52].
